About MankNotes

I read a lot, and I write a lot. Some of it might be useful to people. I think gatekeeping education is one of the worst crimes a society can participate in. It tells those who don’t have access to education that they were never meant to succeed, and it tells those who do that they’re part of a natural elite with the right to take more than others. Both are destructive to knowledge. I aim to keep this as accessible as possible. I will never have a paywall or ask for a subscription to anything here. No promises about ads though. I still need to pay rent.

About Mank

When I was in high school (in the pre-ChatGPT days), one of my teachers caught a handful of students with links containing every past AP test from which questions on the final would be drawn for the entire school. Faced with the dilemma of knowing a handful of students were guaranteed to cheat while everyone else was working hard, this teacher decided to give everyone the link to level the playing field. I actually enjoyed learning, but I could not honestly blame any student in that class for using the link. They tried to maximize their chances of success using methods proven to work by those older than them who got into competitive colleges. The value of knowledge is speculative when you can’t apply any of it for a dozen years. The value of a good grade is reflected in GPAs, class rankings, college admission chances, peer judgment, and the reactions of caregivers. Students are being very rational in recognizing that most students who cheat are never caught while the competition for scarce seats in top colleges continues to get more vicious. We tell them this will define the trajectory of their entire lives.

I was one of two people in my year who took Multivariable Calculus as a high school junior. It was not a weighted class, which meant that even if I aced every assignment and test, it would be mathematically guaranteed to bring down my GPA. The year after, our school made a new version of the class that did seem to be weighted, after which many more students began to take it. We punish curiosity and enthusiasm because they’re not measurable, and then we’re upset when people turn metrics into goals. We can’t have it both ways. At no point in our society have we shown kids the correlation between pursuing knowledge or truth and living a more comfortable life. The opposite, in fact.

We’re just teaching them to calculate the expected value of cheating by evaluating the probability of getting caught along with the benefits of succeeding and costs of failing. Businesses do the same everyday when weighing the cost of complying with regulations against the fines incurred for dodging them. How can we blame our kids for applying that to their lives if that’s the economy they know they’re heading into? I have more than once heard my peers who went on to Ivy League graduate programs state in unambiguous terms that cheating is just part of the rules, and getting away with cheating is a skill like any other. They celebrate their achievements by bragging about how little they had to work or learn compared to others, how they use positions of power to make subordinates do the work they should, how they go to disgustingly lavish dinners where a glass of sparkling water goes into the triple digits, how they network with nepo babies at parties with designer drugs, how they get told their proposals for efficiency lack basic empathy and horrify their peers, how they’re excited to give these advantages to their kids so they can cheat their way to new heights, how their parents hired them tutors and college consultants charging 50k a year. Why wouldn’t they? It’s exactly what’s rewarded in the system they’re in, and they’ve adapted to it better than anyone else.

I’m not better than anyone. I just never had the stomach to swallow all that. I won’t encourage cheating, but I’ll happily do my part to make knowledge as accessible as possible. Sometimes that means I’ll cover a topic that textbooks do a poor job explaining, sometimes that means I’ll try analyzing a movie’s depiction of a serious topic. If I feel an accessible explanation for something doesn’t exist in the world, I consider it worth writing about, however imperfectly or even egregiously. I’ve found that the best way to get a correct answer isn’t by asking questions, it’s by proposing a wrong answer which triggers people into correcting you.

Keep that in mind, because as I said above these are my notes. They’re just notes and rants from one dude without professional credentials. If you disagree completely and think I’m full of crap, that’s great. You know who you are, what you think, and what works in your life. You do not need my approval or agreement, and everything I say is only a perspective you can freely disregard. You will find no claims to absolute truths here.
