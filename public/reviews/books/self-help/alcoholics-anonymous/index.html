Yes, I’m Going There

Look, it’s 2026. It’s been almost nine decades. It’s time we have a conversation about the Alcoholics Anonymous book, or The Big Book as it refers to itself.

This is not a judgment on AA meetings or the community. As with all things relating to life advice, if it works for you, you don’t have to engage with any of my writing. Take what works, leave what doesn’t. I don’t know you, your life, your meetings, your sponsor, your medications, your fellowship, your social network, your medical history, your healthcare access, or anything else relevant to whether AA is helpful or not for your particular life. I could never recommend nor recommend against AA for anyone. I have no idea how it could fit into the portfolio of your recovery capital.

This is solely about a book that is a staple of treatment centers addressing substance abuse disorders and the most common piece of addiction literature recommended around the world. I am not interested in taking away a method that has worked for your life. I’m interested in what this book does for the culture of addiction prevention, perception, and treatment. Most importantly, I’m interested in who it fails for and why.
