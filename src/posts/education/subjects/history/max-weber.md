## Max Weber
If you take AP Government & Politics, you might briefly touch on Weber and his theories about bureaucracy as social organization. You likely haven't read that his ideas used some pretty insane premises.

Max Weber had a theory of personality that framed an American Puritan set of ideals as the most desirable to cultivate and institutionalize. He was seeking to articulate a theory of personality in society that would be characterized by their anti-authoritarianism and autonomy. He tried to survey systems like Hinduism, Buddhism, Confucianism, and Judaism to find the unique aspects of the Puritan personality based on his limited understanding of these societies and their histories. 

From India and China, he interpreted the "Asiatic personality" as traditional and irrational, and therefore incapable of developing into fully autonomous people. He wished that the German education system would try to cultivate the Puritan personality, especially as a solution to developing new political leadership in the wake of a revolution. When we create binaries like this, they tend to permeate and replicate themselves in other constructions of the world. If the ideal political leader was a Puritan Occidental, then the civil servant who should follow this leader with a personality should be a non-personality like his profile of Asiatic (non-)personalities. Sara R. Farris' work on Max Weber is great further reading on this specific pedagogical-political model.

We can certainly credit Weber for advancing ideas about bureaucratic organization from a sociological perspective, but it's strange that we skip over the part that his leader-servant binary was so closely tied to his ideals of Puritan Occidental personality and Asiatic non-personality, and that he explicitly wanted the German educated elite to adopt the former. We can't turn these concepts neutral or "colorblind" by taking an approach that blinds us to why we even include them in syllabi. The presumption of a distinct Western rationality that grew out of some Puritan pedagogical-political structure of society is one that Western tellings of history still promote. 

We like to tell the story of a Puritan Reformation that empowered the individual into literacy, then an Enlightenment movement that articulated rational discourse towards a justification for organizing society, then the First Amendment happened which allowed elites to create a republic that debates ideas openly, then we abolished slavery so that marginalized groups could receive the wonderful Western education that allows them to advocate for themselves under civil rights frameworks, we exported an efficient liberal marketplace to stabilize the world after the chaos that someone caused (we will never know who), then in 2008 we made racism irrelevant thanks to the unique social progressivism of elite colleges, their alumni network, and more educated consumers voting with their wallet. 

We could just as easily tell the story of wealthy families forming corporations to guard and grow their wealth beyond the estate of any single family, which irreparably burned through intellectual capital, trade opportunities, civil peace, and natural resources at a pace never seen before. Slavery has technically never been abolished, only highly regulated. The Nazi lawyers who drafted Nuremberg Laws were actually inspired by the segregation and racial hierarchy of America (some had even studied law in American academia), though many considered it too extreme in its racial divisions. Hitler references the Immigration Act of 1924 in *Mein Kampf* as a good policy to ensure the purity of Northern and Western European families. In 1927, the Supreme Court of the United States issued an opinion that justified forced sterilization: "Three generations of imbeciles is enough." The concept of *lebensraum* as an expansion of German territory was directly compared to the doctrine of *Manifest Destiny* to justify the genocide of indigenous nations. Many high-level Nazi officials considered the US to be their most significant future rival on the basis that both were organized around racial hierarchy and would thus outlive the other nations around them. After WWII, the US certainly grew financially, economically, technologically, and militarily. However, leading social theorists at the time noted that this was the result of being isolated from the rest of the world after a major war and having lots of untapped natural resources to unsustainably extract. Esteemed consultants like Peter Drucker and William Deming noted that America was succeeding despite its management, which was incredibly poor and unable to distinguish between information and knowledge. The veneer of objectivity tossed over metrics and indicators only has the aesthetics of a rational scientific methodology.
