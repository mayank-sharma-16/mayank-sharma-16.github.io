# What is Housing First?
Housing First is one of the most direct and effective ways to solve homelessness: give people housing. Permanent and not conditional on solving other health issues or commitment to an institution first. As the National Alliance to End Homelessness states,

*Housing First is an approach that prioritizes providing permanent housing to people experiencing homelessness, thus ending their homelessness and serving as a platform from which they can pursue personal goals and improve their quality of life. This approach is guided by the belief that people need basic necessities like food and a place to live before attending to anything less critical, such as getting a job, budgeting properly, or attending to substance use issues. Additionally, Housing First is based on the belief that client choice is valuable in housing selection and supportive service participation; exercising that choice is likely to make a client more successful in remaining housed and improving their life. Housing First programs remove barriers faced by households trying to attain permanent housing, and do not require prerequisites to access housing support beyond what is required in a tenant’s lease.*

It's actually quite baffling to me that Housing First is only now getting traction.

If you want to treat health issues that lead to homelessness, you probably should give those patients a place to undergo long-term medical evaluation and treatment. 

If you want to reduce property crimes from people who are worried about rent and basic necessities, it would probably help to reassure them housing programs exist if they get laid off (say, because their bosses jumped the gun on technology that was supposed to replace human employees but hiring the humans back doesn't make up for missed paychecks). 

If you want people to avoid using emergency services as a result of physical and sexual violence, preventable diseases and disorders, exposure to the elements and pandemics, environmental pollution, and other nasty things, it would probably help to take someone out of the place where those things have a higher chance of existing. 

To be blunt, it's an extremely common sense solution that you have to jump through many hoops and bend yourself into many pretzels to ignore. Meta-studies that explore these programs find strong quantitative evidence that it helps individuals stay housed and avoid using emergency resources that are far costlier than preventative measures. 

# Recovery Requires Capital
The science and policy of recovery and addiction treatment has moved away from focusing on total abstinence and CBT/DBT as the gold standard, and instead towards a holistic view of recovery capital. 

*Recovery Capital refers to the internal and external resources that an individual can access to begin and sustain recovery from addiction or mental health conditions. These resources span personal strengths, social networks, and community or cultural supports. Originally introduced by researchers Cloud and Granfield in 1999, Recovery Capital highlights that recovery success depends not only on individual willpower and abstinence but also on the broader social and environmental context. Unlike traditional abstinence-focused measures,  recovery capital through the Recovery Capital Index evaluates the holistic components of wellbeing, enabling a person-centered approach to recovery.*

*Put that way, intellectual capital is some of the lowest-hanging fruit possible to acquire given how plentiful and accessible information has become over the last few decades. Other types of capital still matter, which has been common knowledge since Maslow's hierarchy became a part of K-12 health education programs and directly argued that security must come before purpose or worth.*

After all, if we accept that addiction is a complex biopsychosocial disorder with roots in intergenerational trauma, genetic predisposition, and personal experiences that influence reward mechanisms in the brain, it makes sense to move beyond discussions about knowledge gaps and into capital gaps. 

Recovery Capital is generally split into three types:

- Personal: Physical/Mental/Emotional Wellbeing, Balanced Nutrition, Employment Status, Job Satisfaction, Workplace Support, Formal Education, Growth Opportunities, Financial Stability and Stressors, Stable and Safe Housing, Transportation Accessibility, Clothing Availability

- Social: Emotional and Practical Family Assistance, Relationship Support from a Significant Other, Friendships, Comfort and Aid Networks, Professional and Personal Mobility, Wellness Activities, Support Groups, Access to Healthcare, Community Safety

- Cultural: Alignment and Respect for Beliefs, Clarity and Strength of Principles, Integration of Spiritual Practices, Meaning & Purpose, Culturally Appropriate Support Groups, Feelings of Community Belonging

Although called "recovery" capital, the many dimensions to one's health that are discovered in the process of recovery are applicable to practically anyone. We can see how complex of an issue addiction is when there's so much overlap and intersection from the types of capital above. This isn't a new idea inspired by social justice either; this was identified as a model in 1999.
