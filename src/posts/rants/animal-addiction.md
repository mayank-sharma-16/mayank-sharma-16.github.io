Humans are far from the only animals that can experience addictive compulsions and behaviors.

## Dogs

Here's a study about "excessive toy motivation" in dogs as a parallel to process addictions in humans like gambling. "Our results highlight parallels between excessive toy motivation in dogs and human behavioural addictions, with dogs as the only non-human species so far that appears to develop addictive-like behaviours spontaneously without artificial induction." Man's best friend, truly. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-18636-0 Some have taken to calling these dogs "ball junkies."

Seriously though, it's interesting that the animal we've domesticated to be most compatible with our understanding of reinforcement-based training also presents with "addiction-like behavior." Dog brains also use dopamine reward pathways that can be disrupted by addictive stimulus.

It's also seriously dangerous for the dogs in question. It's not an exaggeration.

The conventional criteria observed in human addiction (craving, salience, and loss of self-control) are also observed in these dogs. It's possible for dogs to prioritize the toy over food, rest, and socialization. In extreme cases, dogs can chase toys to the point of exhaustion, joint injury, or heat stroke. When the toy is taken away, the dogs show measurable distress like elevated heart rates and persistent vigiliance. There are even genetic components. Working breeds like Border Collies and Belgian Malinois are more prone to developing these addictive-like behaviors.

Dogs can develop chemical dependencies too, though usually they aren't exposed to addictive chemicals regularly in a domestic context. If they are, they can also go through withdrawal with symptoms like tremors, agitation, and vomiting. 

In some places where toxic wildlife is common, dogs can end up using them for psychoactive effects. Dogs that lick certain cane toads ingest bufotenine, which can cause hallucinations, and they can end up compulsively hunting for these toads even after feeling sick. Dropped fruit that ferments can produce alcohol that wild dogs are known to return to. 

Prescription medication can impact dogs just like humans. Benzodiazepines prescribed for separation anxiety or phenobarbital prescribed for epilepsy can induce dependency. If they're abruptly stopped, digs can experience fatal withdrawals with tremors and seizures.

Although arguably closer to OCD than addiction, compulsive behaviors even extend to self-harm. Some dogs with Canine Compulsive Disorder (CCD) can cause serious self-injury by repeatedly chewing at themselves, swallowing inedible items, or skipping meals and sleep to continue the behavior.
