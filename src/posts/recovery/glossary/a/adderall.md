# Overview
Adderall is a central nervous system (CNS) stimulant that uses mixed amphetamine salts to increase dopamine and norepinephrine levels in the brain. It's most commonly used to treat attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and narcolepsy, usually through improving focus and impulse control. In the United States, adderall is classified as a Schedule II drug as it has high potential for becoming part of a substance use disorder, so it requires a prescription for controlled doses that stabilize chemistry rather than producing a high. Even if taken exactly as prescribed, over a long period of time, the body can adapt to it so that sudden cessation leads to withdrawal symptoms like fatigue and depression.
