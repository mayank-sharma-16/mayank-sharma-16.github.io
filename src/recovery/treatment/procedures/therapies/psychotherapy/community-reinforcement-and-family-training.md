---
title: Community Reinforcement and Family Training (CRAFT)
slug: registry/treatment/modalities/community-reinforcement-and-family-training-craft
---

The intuition behind CRAFT is that loved ones know a lot about the person in their life who is struggling in ways that professionals never will, but they may not know how to apply that into encouraging a reduction in substance use, treatment that explores abstinence, or setting up boundaries that respect everyone's needs. Professionals can teach those skills in the abstract when someone enters their clinic or classroom, but loved ones have the ability to assess someone in the context of their unique lived experiences together. 

CRAFT can change someone's environment while they're still deliberating about their substance use and treatment options; invitations to talk are considered more welcoming than confrontations with ultimatums. At the same time, CRAFT also helps loved ones assess and respect their own needs in order to be a long-term sustainable supporter, including setting boundaries around enabling the consequences of drug use without punity. 

A 1999 study showed only 1 out of 10 Al-Anon attendees succeeded at introducing their loved one to treatment. That's arguably not the goal of Al-Anon, but it is one of the most recommended venues to have these conversations. Other notable points of comparison include no overt spiritual or religious frameworks, so no emphasis on higher powers, powerlessness, or stepwork that can turn off those outside the AA Buchmanist umbrella.
