title: Cesare Lombroso
---

Cesare Lombroso is both considered a founder of modern criminology and one of the most prolific eugenicists we've seen. Rather than seeing crimes and sin as part of human nature, he believed some people were born with a criminal aptitude, which he theorized was indicative of some reversion of humanity to primitive primates. He thought these natural-born criminals could be identified through physical features, and even the types of crimes they would commit could be determined. He also coined the term "criminaloid" to refer to those who conceal a "criminal personality" underneath a projection of respectability.

Lombroso's theories ran into empirical problems even his day, but he stubbornly held fast to them. For instance, he believed men were superior to women, but this didn't square with women being involved in crime far less than men. He actually managed a mental institution himself and became the first professor to specialize in mental health at the University of Pavia. Italian universities did not remove his school of thought from the curriculums until after WWII, because predictably, fascist movements thrived on the aesthetics of scientific legitimacy he provided.

This had nuanced implications for legal systems and policies. Lombroso and his school of biological determinists actually argued that criminality should be understood in the context of biological predisposition to crime, and he advocated for socialist ideas that expressed sympathy for the lower classes suffering from the stigma of poverty. In a way, by arguing that certain people were biologically limited from full participation in society, he was advocating for more lenience than the classical schools of his predecessors. As as aside, I was watching The Gambler just before writing this, and I'm reminded of the monologue Mark Wahlberg's character gives about the rage we experience at the unequal distribution of human talent. Someone with every privilege possible can end up completely mediocre in everything they try, while someone born with every disadvantage can be outright prodigious in a way that can be observed at the age of two. That kind of stochasticity is upsetting to people, even those who benefit from the luck of the draw. It's a different kind of unfairness to realize that you can give your child every advantage possible as a result of your work ethic and judgment, but you can't do anything about whether your child is predisposed to a talent you could guide or nurture. It's also similarly uncomfortable to realize that you have access to a talent or perspective no one else does for no discernible reason. You're different and you don't know why, and you're probably going to experience the world differently. That can be lonely and ostracizing in its own way. Even really bad pseudoscience can become a strongly internalized rationalization to fill the gaps.
