# Overview
"Cinchona" describes a genus of plants within the coffee family whose bark is a source of quinine and quinidine, which have historically been used to treat treatment-resistant strains of malaria, heart arrhythmias, and nocturnal leg muscle cramping. 

Some commercial products like tonic water contain small amounts of quinine that is known to add a bitter taste. However, in high quantities, often found in unregulated supplements, it's toxic enough to produce a syndrome called cinchonism, characterized by:

- tinnitus, or a persistent ringing in the ears

- headaches

- dizziness

- altered vision

- nausea

- heart block

- fatal arrhythmias

- hearing loss

- thrombocytopenia, or a drop in blood platelets

- miscarriage

# History and Etymology
By 1944, the technology to develop synthetic quinine led to a decline in large-scale cultivation of the plant, though it remains significant culturally (e.g. as the national tree of Peru.)
