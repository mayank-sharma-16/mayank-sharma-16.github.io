# Overview
"Cold turkey" is an informal phrase that references attempts to quit substance use suddenly at once rather than gradually reducing usage over time. 

With some substances like alcohol and [[benzodiazepines|benzodiazepines]], this can be fatal, so it's almost never a good idea to quit cold turkey. It really is gambling with your life, and most medically-managed detoxification protocols will make your experience vastly more comfortable than quitting at once. If quitting at home or on one's own, a better idea is to gradually taper off drinking patterns, with a rule of thumb being to decrease the total amount of drinks consumed per day by 2. That being said, I am not a doctor and have no medical training, so I cannot give medical advice and nothing on this page or site should be construed as such.

Even outside of alcohol and benzos, quitting cold turkey can be a miserable experience. Modern medicine has come up with some really helpful solutions to managing some of the symptoms of withdrawal, and I highly encourage you to take advantage of them instead of trying to tough it out. There are no extra credit points given for getting sober on your own without help.
