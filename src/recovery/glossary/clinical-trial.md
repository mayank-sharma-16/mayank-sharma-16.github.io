# Overview
A clinical trial is a research experiment that tests medical or behavioral procedures in humans to measure effectiveness and detect risks.

Clinical trials in modern scientific institutions generally follow four phases:

1. An investigation in a small group of people, typically less than 100, to scope out the ranges for safe dosages and document side effects to look at.

2. A larger trial on 100-300 people to begin tracking treatment effectiveness.

3. A trial with hundreds or thousands of patients to obtain coverage across multiple regions and monitor side effects in a diverse selection of people

4. Monitoring long-term safety and effects across a population after approval has been granted
