# Overview
"Affective world" (also referred to as "affective landscape", "affective environment", and "Umwelt") describes the subjective emotional filter through which every piece of reality is interpreted.

For instance, "I am safe" might be a subconscious baseline in an affective world in which trauma is absent or managed well, whereas "I am under constant danger" might be the baseline for someone who has unresolved negative impacts from past trauma. In the first person's affective world, well-intentioned criticism might only lead to fleeting irritation. In the second person's affective world, the criticism might be seen as preceding a threat of abandonment or discipline. Another example is social anxiety. To the first person, a crowded room might be seen as energizing with many opportunities to socialize. To the second person, the room might seem like a place where threats could come from any direction, so they might scan for exit routes or more secluded spaces.
