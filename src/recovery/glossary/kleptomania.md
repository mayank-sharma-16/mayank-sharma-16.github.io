# Overview
Kleptomania is a mental health disorder ni which someone has a recurring urge to steal items without personal gain as a motivator. 

While it's often considered a behavioral addiction along the lines of gambling or sex addictions, the [[diagnostic-and-statistical-manual-of-mental-disorders|DSM-5-TR]] technically categorizes it as an impulse-control disorder. The underlying mechanisms for kleptomania are almost all shared with other addictions. Much like gambling leads to a spike in dopamine in the tense anticipation of an outcome that engages the brain's attention, stealing can also trigger the same spikes due to the tension leading up to the theft and gratification while doing it. The item itself is not the source of the brain's recognition of reward, the same way that addictive gambling is about the act rather than the money gained. At a neurobiological level, the [[endogenous|endogenous]] opioid system in the brain, which regulates urges and self-control, is thought to suffer from imbalances that make it difficult to resist the temptation to steal. Naltrexone, an opioid antagonist that is used to curb cravings for alcohol and opioid addiction, is also effective at curbing the urges to steal found in kleptomania. 

It's common for people suffering from kleptomania to return or otherwise get rid of the items they stole out of guilt and shame. You may have heard a story, online or from a friend talking about a friend of a friend, about someone going to a party and stealing family photo albums because they're not replaceable even if they're financially worthless. That's the stereotype of an unapologetic kleptomaniac, but it shouldn't be extrapolated to people who have the disorder in general.  



# Commentary
I've heard some people complain that Marie's kleptomania arc in *Breaking Bad* was unnecessary or pointless aside from expanding the world beyond the White household a bit. I actually love it. Not every instance of pathologized compulsion leads to death or cinematic consequences. Sometimes it just interferes with your life and relationship in ways that are still distressing and worthy of addressing with medical help.
