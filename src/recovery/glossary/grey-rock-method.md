# Overview
Grey rocking, or the grey rock method, is the practice of making oneself as non-response and non-reactive as possible in order to avoid giving manipulative individuals the reactions or attention they're looking for. This can look like:

- One- or two-word answers like "yes," "no," and "okay" that try to end the conversation as soon as possible

- Limiting emotional expressions like keeping a neutral or stiff facial expression, avoiding eye contact, and keeping a flat affect in one's voice

- Limiting personal discussions that include your private life and future plans

- Avoid taking the bait, which includes refusing to defend yourself in an argument from accusations or insults

# History and Etymology
The term was coined in a blog called 180 Rule, authored by someone who went only by Skylar, which is no longer a live site but can still be browsed through archives:

*"When dealing with malignant narcissists, psychopaths, sociopaths, borderlines, drama queens, stalkers and other emotional vampires, it’s commonly advised that no response is the best response to unwanted attention. This is often true and No Contact (the avoidance of all communication) should be used whenever possible."*

*"There are some situations however, when No Contact is not feasible, as in when you share child custody with a psychopath. As another example, if you are being stalked by an ex, a restraining order can infuriate the unwanted suitor, and refusing to respond to him or her is seen as an insult. They might become convinced that they can MAKE you respond and in that way satiate their need for power over you."*

*"For all these situations, we have the Gray Rock Method."*

I don't necessarily like the terminology used to describe those that grey rocking might be useful for, but the advice is solid. In particular, "borderlines" is a dehumanizing way to refer to people who have Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD), which is very distinct from the stereotypes of "psychopaths" or "sociopaths." People who struggle with BPD deal with emotional dysregulation and strong fears of abandonment, not a lack of empathy or remorse that can lead to predatory or manipulative behaviors. That doesn't necessarily mean the advice can't work when it comes to protecting oneself from dynamics that aren't healthy for them, but dehumanizing the other party isn't a prerequisite to developing those coping mechanisms.
