# Overview
"Grounding" in a psychological context refers to coping mechanisms and techniques that can disrupt dissociative episodes, oncoming flashbacks, hypervigilance and hyperarousal, and anxiety or panic attacks by interrupting these symptoms to redirect awareness towards the present. These include:

- The 5-4-3-2-1 Method: Name 5 things you can see, 4 things you can feel, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, and 1 thing you can taste. This addresses each of the senses with a new sensation to focus on.

- Temperature Shock: Splash cold water on your face, hold ice cubs in your hand, or run warm water over your hands and wrist to shock the nervous system into processing a new sensation.

- Axial Foot Pressing: Sit down and press both feet into the floor while focusing on the sesnsations of pressure between the bottom of your feet and the floor.

- The 4-7-8 Breathing Method: Inhale through the nose for 4 seconds, hold your breath for 7 seconds, and exhale through the mouth for 8 seconds. There's a common warning given with this one as being potentially risky for those who struggle with sudden changes in blood pressure.

- Texture Tracking: Run your fingers over some object near you while describing physical properties about it the best you can, like texture, weight, and temperature

- Progressive Muscle Relaxation: Slowly clench, flex, or tense parts of your body for a few seconds each and release the tension, focusing on physical stress leaving the body as muscles relax.

- Anchoring Statements: Repeat facts about the present out loud, like "My name is Mank, and I am safely resting in my room while surrounded by people who care in the year 2026."

- Mathematical Disruption: Repeat some kind of formula or pattern in your head like counting backward from 100 by 7s, calculating the Fibonacci sequence, or repeating multiplication tables.

- Alphabetical Categorization: Choose a category of things and name one item for each letter in the alphabet in sorted order.

- Spatial Inventory: Examine the room or environment you're in while counting each object of a particular color or shape.

- Spelling Reversal: Spell long words backwards out loud as fast as you can.
