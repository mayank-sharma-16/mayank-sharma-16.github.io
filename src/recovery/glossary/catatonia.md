# Overview
Catatonia is a syndrome common in mood disorders like major depressive disorders that involves a severely withdrawn affect and difficulty processing information, sometimes appearing to be motionless or repeating certain movements. 

The ICD-11 and DSM-5 both recognized catatonia based on a list of criteria, three of which must be met:

- Agitation: restlessness or excessive movements

- Echolalia: repeating another person's verbal speech patterns

- Echopraxia: repeating another person's physical movements

- Grimacing: holding facial expressions that are disconnected from normal or appropriate emotions in their environment

- Stupor: appearing to be immobile and unresponsive

- Catalepsy: holding a posture or gesture despite gravity 

- Waxy flexibility: slight resistance to adjustments to movements

- mutism: lack of verbal responsiveness despite appearing to be alert

- negativism: resistance or unresponsiveness to instructions or external stimulus

- posturing: voluntarily adopting unusual posture

- mannerism: exaggerated movements

- stereotypy: repetitive movements or gestures that don't serve any purpose
