# Overview
Under the Schema Therapy model advanced by Jeffrey Young, an early maladaptive schema (EMS) is a recurring pattern or core belief that develops in childhood or adolescence that serves as a source of dysfunction throughout one's life. Generally, these schemas are rooted in formative experiences or trauma and are strongly held in a way that shapes new information to reinforce them.

Following the [[young-schema-questionnaire-revised|Young Schema Questionnaire-Revised (YSQ-R)]], clinicians generally acknowledge 20 schemas:

- Abandonment or Instability

- Mistrust or Abuse

- Emotional Deprivation

- Defectiveness or Shame

- Social Isolation or Alienation

- Dependence or Incompetence

- Vulnerability to Harm or Illness

- Enmeshment or Underdeveloped Self

- Failure

- Entitlement or Grandiosity

- Insufficient Self-Control or Self-Discipline

- Subjugation

- Self-Sacrifice

- Approval-Seeking or Recognition-Seeking

- Negativity or Pessimism

- Emotional Constriction

- Fear of Losing Control

- Unrelenting Standards or Hypercriticalness

- Punitive-Self

- Punitiveness-Others
