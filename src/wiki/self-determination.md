# Overview
Self-determination is the ability to make choices without being coerced or compelled by external factors. In political science, it often refers to the ability of a group of people to decide how they want to structure their communities and political structures. In psychology, self-determination is applied to an individual's ability to make choices about their own life.

Self-Determination Theory argues that humans have three psychological needs underlying their motivation: autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Intrinsic motivation means doing something because the action is personally engaging or meaningful. Extrinsic motivation means doing something for external rewards or to avoid external consequences. Intrinsic motivation is the result of having those three needs met, otherwise extrinsic motivation becomes the driver of our behavior.
