# Overview
The idea of "tough love" is to use firm discipline, boundaries, and consequences to promote growth. There's a lot of variation in both how people define "tough love" and how people feel about "tough love."

In the best-case scenario, tough love involves applying consistent rules fairly but firmly. The tendency to take shortcuts by bending rules or skipping self-discipline is curbed by enforcement with consequences. Someone who uses tough love should ideally have the same compassionate intentions as someone who prioritizes forgiveness and harm reduction; they just have a different idea of what is most effective in helping others.

In the worst-case scenario, tough love can just be a euphemism for toxic dynamics or outright abuse under the belief that suffering builds character. Stigma around mental health issues, or at least patterns that Western psychiatry would recognize as pathologies, can be found in virtually every culture in the world. People in recovery often need a validating, supportive, and collaborative environment in order to safely work through the root causes behind their maladaptive coping mechanisms. Experiencing harm in environments that should be safe and reward vulnerability can alienate someone from seeking help from medical institutions.
