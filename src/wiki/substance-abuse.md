# Overview
"Substance abuse" is another term for [[substance-misuse|"substance misuse"]] or "substance use disorder." It was constructed relatively recently in the history of modern psychiatry, but it's already being phased out by medical institutions, regulatory agencies, and research communities.

# Historical Context
The term "substance abuse" was introduced in the DSM-III in 1980 and replaced "addiction," since alcohol problems were viewed as a part of "Sociopathic Personality Disturbances" in earlier editions of the DSM that drew on Freudian psychodynamics. In the third edition, there was a new category called "Substance Use Disorders" that contained both Substance Dependence (for those showing physiological symptoms) and Substance Abuse (for those who had a pattern of usage that interfered with life without necessarily a dependence). 

By the time the DSM-IV was published in 1994, substance abuse was seen as a milder form or early-stage version of substance dependence, which was the result of chronic substance abuse. Institutions like the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) have adopted the term into their brand and articulation of their mission.

In 2013, with the publication of the DSM-5, "substance abuse" was eliminated as a label, and the diagnosis was merged into a single continuum called Substance Use Disorder (SUD). Organizations like the National Institute on Drug Abuse (NIDA) have mandated terms like "substance misuse" or "SUD" over "substance abuse." Researchers found evidence that the inclusion of the term "abuse" triggered negative biases even among medical professionals due to its association with contexts like abusive relationships.
