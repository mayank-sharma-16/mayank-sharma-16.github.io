# Overview
Person-first language, or people-first language, is a way of phrasing language in a way that emphasizes the individual rather than a condition or a trait. One of the most common examples of that you'll find on this site is the use of "person  struggling with substance use disorder" rather than "addict." 

Medical and research communities tend to use person-first language to maximize inclusivity, especially as studies have shown that language choices do make a difference in the subjective margins of even professional judgment. Clinicians and researchers who encounter the label "substance abuser" are more likely to have punitive attitudes than those who encounter the label "person with a substance use disorder." There's always a fuss any time people are asked to update their language outside of organic trends, but the evidence shows that there's more than just theoretical or ideological harm being done with language.
