# Overview
A psychogenic disease refers to a medical condition that originates in the mind, as a more specific type of psychosomatic disease. 

Psychogenic Disease used to be a diagnosis in the DSM-IV. It referred to a condition in which mental stress can lead to physical symptoms, often those of an existing disorder. It was also called "conversion disorder," though the term was phased out for the DSM-5. There's an binary implication in psychogenic that splits factors purely between physical and mental realms, when a better analogy is that the brain's software and hardware are miscommunicating. Instead, the DSM-5-TR offers Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder (FND) and Somatic Symptom Disorder (SSD), and the ICD-11 offers Dissociative Neurological Symptom Disorder and Bodily Distress Disorder (BDD).
