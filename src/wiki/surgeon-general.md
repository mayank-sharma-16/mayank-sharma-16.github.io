# Overview
The United States Surgeon General is the chief public health officer of the U.S. federal government and head of the U.S. Public Health Service Commissioned Corps.

# Historical Context
In 1964, Surgeon General Luther Terry published a report linking cigarettes to lung cancer, which led to mandatory warning labels in 1965 and bans on TV advertising in 1970. However, Terry's described the use of tobacco as a "habit" rather than an "addiction."

In 2016, Surgeon General Vivek Murthy released *Facing Addiction in America: The Surgeon General's Report on Alcohol, Drugs, and Health,* which was the first report in history from the Surgeon General dedicated to substance use patterns and disorders.
