# Overview
Peer recovery describes frameworks for mutual support among those who share lived experiences or shared identity, particularly in mental health and substance use recovery contexts. Public stigma, scientific limitations, and abuses of formal institutions have all been major factors in the growth of peer-led recovery groups and services all over the world. Evidence shows that including peer support into a treatment plan increases engagement in treatment, reduces the risk of readmission, decreases substance use and overdoses, and improves feelings of self-sufficiency. 

Peer recovery specialists are jobs where lived experience with recovery is actually a useful qualification, and they usually involve formal certification with a training program and exams. These are distinct roles from addiction counselors and therapists.
