# Overview
Mass incarceration refers to the practice of imprisoning large amounts of people, and the term is generally used in reference to the War on Drugs in United States. 

Despite having only 5% of the global population, the United States holds almost 25% of the world's prisoners. 65% of those in prisons have a substance use disorder, and 47% had a substance use disorder in the year before their entry. Many groups of color are overrepresented in the prison population, with Black Americans making up about 33% despite being 14% of the general US population and having comparable rates of drug use with white populations. 

Prisons are not generally equipped to address substance use disorder as a genuine medical condition. Many who are incarcerated are suddenly thrust into withdrawals without adequate medical care to manage them. Rehabilitation requires proper treatment to break a cycle of relapse and recidivism through expensive emergency services.
