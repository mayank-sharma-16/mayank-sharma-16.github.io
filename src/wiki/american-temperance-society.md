# Overview
The American [[temperance|Temperance]] Society (ATS), or the American Society for the Promotion of Temperance, was the first national social movement in the United States to mobilize support for social reforms. They started out in 1826 with Lyman Beecher and Justin Edwards leading a group opposed to the consumption of hard liquor, but eventually grew to favor the [[prohibitionism|prohibition]] of all forms of alcohol through [[abstinence|abstinence]], or [[teetotaler|teetotalism]]. By 1835, their membership grew to 1.5 million people with more than 8000 local groups. 

In 1836, in part to quell arguments from factions against total abstinence, the ATS merged into the American Temperance Union. The movement pushed for legal restrictions on alcohol consumption, like the 1851 Maine law. They were heavily disrupted by the American Civil War (1861-1865) and dissolved completely in 1866.
