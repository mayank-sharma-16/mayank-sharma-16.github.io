# Overview
Oncology is the branch of medicine that focuses on cancer. 

Substance use and cancer have a bidirectional relationship. Many substances increase the rate of developing cancer, cancer treatment can involve medications that are addictive, and someone can have co-occurring substance use disorders that can lead to different recommendations for a course of treatment.

According to the American Cancer Society, 80% of all lung cancer deaths involve tobacco use, and 30% of all cancer deaths involve combined tobacco and alcohol use. [[intravenous|Intravenous]] drug use can increase the risk of contracting Hepatitis B and C, which can cause liver cancer. 

Because cancer can cause severe pain, many patients are prescribed opioids like fentanyl and oxycodone, and so they may develop a dependence that resembles but is distinct from what we conventionally consider a substance use disorder. Others really will develop substance use disorders, or relapse if they already struggled with using substances.
