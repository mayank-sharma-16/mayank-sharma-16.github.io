# Overview
Training and technical assistance (TTA) describes specialized support services that teach individuals and organizations to implement new evidence-based practices, increase operational efficiency, and improve strategic planning. 

In substance use recovery and treatment, TTA is a vital part of translating evidence from the latest research and trials into practice. There's actually an entire field for this process called implementation science. One of the biggest examples of TTA infrastructure in the United States is the [[addiction-technology-transfer-center-network|Addiction Technology Transfer Center (ATTC) Network]], which provides TTA to professionals for SUD treatment. The Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) manages the ATTC, as well as the Prevention Techology Transfer Centers (PTTC) and Mental Health Technology Transfer Centers (MHTTC) for co-occurring mental health disorders. More recently, TTA from these centers has involved training emergency responders and clincians to handle high-potency substances like [[fentanyl|fentanyl]] and severe [[withdrawal|withdrawals]].

# Historical Context
In the United States, the federal government began using the term "Training and Technical Assistance" as part of the Economic Opportunity Act under the War on Poverty in 1965. Policymakers had realized that giving grant money to local communities and leaving them to figure out the operational details was highly ineffective, leading to duplicated efforts in siloed bureaucracies. TTA networks emerged to help local agencies and programs meet federal standards by providing training and operational support on top of grants.
