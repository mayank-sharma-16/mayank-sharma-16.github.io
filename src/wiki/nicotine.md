# Overview
Nicotine is a naturally occurring alkaloid found in the leaves of tobacco plants that acts as a [[stimulant|stimulant]]. Although it's not generally classified as a [[carcinogen|carcinogen]] itself, it's still an addictive substance that keeps people using tobacco products and consuming other substances alongside their nicotine. 

The short-term effects of nicotine include elevated heart rate and blood pressure, temporary focus, reduced anxiety, appetite suppression, nausea, lightheadedness, etc. The long-term effects include harming adolescent brain development, raising the risk of heart disease and stroke, and interference with blood sugar regulation which can increase the risk of developing [[diabetes|diabetes]].

Nicotine is often used as a safer alternative to tobacco, but it's by no means risk-free. Nicotine is the addictive compound in tobacco, but the toxic chemicals and heavy metals found in the smoke from tobacco is what drives cancer and lung disease. However, the other symptoms like heart disease and insulin resistance still occur when using nicotine alone.
