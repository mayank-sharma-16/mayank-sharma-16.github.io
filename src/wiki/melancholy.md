# Overview
In casual usage, melancholy refers to a deep or prolonged sadness that can involve a sense of introspection or self-reflection. 

Historically, melancholia was a term from Ancient Greek that translates literally to "black bile" and traced emotional disturbances from seemingly irrational beliefs to an imbalance of fluids inside someone. Humorism declined through advancements in science during the 18th century, but melancholia continued to be used as a diagnosis for the symptoms that we might now attribute to depression and anxiety. As of the DSM-III, the term "melancholia" no longer served as an independent condition but instead as a specifier for a severe version of Major Depressive Disorder.
