# Overview
Synchronicity is the experience of believing that two events happening at the same time has personally symbolic significance. In other words, it refers to a coincidence that someone has ascribed meaning to. Although it's not considered a testable or measurable concept that can be empirically validated through research, many clinicians and therapists find that the concept is helpful anyways.

In recovery, the concept of synchronicity shows up in many places. Carl Jung first coined the term in psychology, and he was also a correspondent of William Wilson (Bill W.), one of the founders of Alcoholics Anonymous (AA). In one letter to Wilson, Jung notes the synchronicity found in the Latin word for alcohol being *spiritus* while the word for spiritual connection is *spiritum:* "*spiritus contra spiritum.* If one does not find spiritual connection, one uses alcohol to experience an artificial spiritual connection. Finding that authentic connection might itself be caused by synchronicity that inspires one to reevaluate their way of experiencing the world. AA draws heavily on these ideas, which is exemplified in Steps 11 and 12. Step 11 instructs members to contemplate the will of their [[higher-power|Higher Power]], which is done by searching the events in one's life for deeper meaning. Step 12 describes explicitly that the completion of the Steps ought to have led to a [[spiritual-awakening|"spiritual awakening."]] Members might practice exercises like repeating self-affirmations, making gratitude lists, and reading recovery literature to help find connections that identify areas for personal and spiritual growth.

# Historical Context
The term was first introduced by Carl Jung in the early 20th century.
