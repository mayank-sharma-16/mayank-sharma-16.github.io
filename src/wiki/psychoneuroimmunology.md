# Overview 
Psychoneuroimmunology (PNI), also called psychoendoneuroimmunology (PNEI), is the study of the intersection of psychological processes, the nervous system, the endocrine system, and the immune system. In simple terms, the brain and the immune system influence each other through hormones.

One of the biggest problems being researched in the field is the role of inflammation in mental health. The cytokine hypothesis of depression proposes that systemic inflammation across the blood-brain barrier changes the metabolism of neurotransmitters. Cytokines that provoke inflammation reduce serotonin and contribute to Major Depressive Disorder (MDD). Chronic stress can cause neuroinflammation in the brain's immune cells, called microglia, which can interfere with neuroplasticity in the [[prefrontal-cortex|prefrontal cortex]] and hippocampus. Decreased neuroplasticity can result in more rigid thought patterns that are difficult to change, a hallmark of clinical depression. Inflammation can form a vicious cycle too; chronic stress means the body is constantly being flooded with cortisol that desensitizes the immune system to its own brakes.
