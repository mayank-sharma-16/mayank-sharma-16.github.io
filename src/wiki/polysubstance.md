# Overview
Polysubstance use refers to using more than one substance within a short window of time. This can happen intentionally when users try to enhance or neutralize the effects of one substance with another, but it can also happen unintentionally when substances have been contaminated or cut with other substances.

Some categories of drug interactions include:

- Depressant + Depressant: Combining opioids, alcohol, [[benzodiazepines|benzodiazepines]], and muscle relaxants can compound the central nervous system and respiratory depression in ways that dramatically increase the risk of hypoxia, sedation, coma, and respiratory failure.

- Stimulant + Depressant: Stimulants are sometimes used to mask the sedative effects of a depressant, or vice versa, but stimulants are often short-acting. This means that the depressive effects of higher doses can suddenly kick in when the stimulants wear off, which can cause cardiac dysrhythmia and strokes. Some combinations like cocaine and alcohol cause the liver to produce uniquely toxic [[metabolite|metabolites]] like cocaethylene.

- Stimulant + Stimulant: Stacking stimulants can over-activate the systems of neurotransmitters like dopamine, serotonin, and norepinephrine. This can lead to hypertension, hyperthermia, tachyarrhythmias, and seizures.

- Serotoneregic Combinations: This combination can lead to serotonin syndrome, which presents with hyperreflexia, autonomic instability, muscle rigidity, high fever, and delirium.

- Dissociative/Psychedelic + Cannabis: Cannabis changes the way other substances are processed, which increases the chances of panic attacks, paranoia, depersonalization, or psychosis
