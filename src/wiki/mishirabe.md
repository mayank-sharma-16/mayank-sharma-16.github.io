# Overview
Mishirabe is a Buddhist practice of meditation through sensory and physical deprivation, originating in the traditions of the Jodo Shinshu (Pure Land) branch of Japanese Buddhism. Practitioners would enter a small isolated space like a dark room or a cave for long periods of time - sometimes more than a week. Driving the body and mind to exhaustion was meant to strip away defense mechanisms and rationalizations to facilitate a realization of the boundless forces sustaining someone in spite of their ego. Because it was such an intense practice, Ishin Yishimoto, a businessman and follower of Jodo Shinshu practices, derived a simpler, non-sectarian method known as [[naikan|Naikan Therapy]], which is now widely used across clinical settings in Japan and parts of Europe.
