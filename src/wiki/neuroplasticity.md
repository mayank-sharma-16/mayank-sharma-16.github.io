# Overview
Neuroplasticity is the brain's ability to change its neural circuits throughout its life. Structural plasticity refers to the ability of the brain to physically alter its connections, while functional plasticity refers to the ability to move functions to different regions. These mechanisms include: 

- Long-Term Potentiation (LTP): neural circuits that are used more often become stronger with faster and more efficient signal transmission

- Synaptic Pruning: rarely used neural connections weaken and disappear to save energy for other parts of the network

- Neurogenesis: new neurons are continually generated throughout one's life, though the rate of generation slows down as one gets older
