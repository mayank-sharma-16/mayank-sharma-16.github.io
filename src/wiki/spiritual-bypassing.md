---

# Overview
Spiritual bypassing is the practice of using spiritual ideas to avoid emotional or mental pain. 

"Good vibes only" encapsulates the concept well, as do other slogans that use toxic positivity, where something might be dismissed purely because it's uncomfortable. Ideas like enlightenment or non-attachment aren't meant to eliminate grief, anger, or fear through dissociation, but to accept them as parts of the human experience and allow them to be processed. Other examples include "everything happens for a reason," "nothing really matters," and "failure always comes before success." That's not to say these platitudes can't be used appropriately as part of healthy coping mechanisms or advice, but they can also just be used to dismiss someone having a normal spectrum of human emotions. 

# Commentary
Anecdotally, I've noticed a lot of people in recovery at some point began to believe that anger and grief are "bad" emotions that are the result of [[character-defects|character defects]], like attempting to control things outside of oneself. Spiritual bypassing can be a coping mechanism people use to avoid feeling "bad" emotions and repress them instead. In some cultures, even expressing hate for God is seen as a meditative activity that still explores the relationship between one and the universe.
