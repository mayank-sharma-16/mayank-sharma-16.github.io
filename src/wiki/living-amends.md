# Overview
In 12-step recovery circles, "living amends" refers to living life with an attitude of making amends towards the world around you, especially in response to being unable to make personal amends with someone. The Oxford Group from which Alcoholics Anonymous (AA) arose laid out Four Practices: sharing, surrender, restitution, and guidance. When Bill Wilson was adapting the Oxford Group's spiritual principles for AA, he changed "restitution" to "amends" to emphasize that much of the damage that is done in the struggles of addiction is emotional and mental rather than just loans or property damage that could be paid back. Since it's impossible to make amends with someone who passed away or may have strict boundaries against contacting them, the idea of "living amends" allows one to work through the guilt by changing their actions and behaviors in a way that honors the amends that one would wish to make.
