# Overview
Reconsolidation in the context of psychology refers to the process in which a long-term memory is retrieved, updated, and restabilized. 

The process first involves reactivation, in which a past memory or unconscious schema is invoked. If one experiences something that contradicts the prediction suggested by the past memory, the nervous system can process the new data and update the pattern of the old memory. However, if the nervous system is not calm enough to begin reconsolidation, it can trigger defense mechanisms or panic at the distress of reality being out of sync with one's expectations. Modalities like EMDR rely on reconsolidation of past trauma in orer to weaken their influence on someone's actions and reactions to triggers.
