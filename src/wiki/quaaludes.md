# Overview
Quaaludes are synthetic central nervous system (CNS) [[depressant|depressants]] that were once prescribed for insomnia and [[anxiety|anxiety]]. Because of its high potential for dependence and fatal respiratory depression, especially when mixed with alcohol, it was eventually moved to a Schedule II controlled substance in 1973 and a Schedule I drug in 1984. They're also associated with enabling sexual assault through their amnesiac and sleep-inducing properties when slipped into a drink.
