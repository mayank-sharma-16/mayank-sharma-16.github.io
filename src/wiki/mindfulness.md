# Overview
Mindfulness refers to maintaining present awareness of thoughts, feelings, and sensations without judgment. Although it's something of a marketing term that gets easily conflated with authenticity or focus, it has roots in South Asian traditions and was brought into mainstream Western popular culture through a revival of interest in Buddhism during New Age spiritual movements. 

Common mindfulness exercises include:

- 5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Grounding: Look at 5 things you can see, 4 things you can feel, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, and 1 thing you can taste

- Sequential Body Scan: shift attention from the toes to the top of the head to notice the sensations without trying to change them

- Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR): tense muscle groups for 5 seconds or so at a time, and then release the tension for 10 seconds or so at a time

- Thought Labeling: when a thought or emotion arises, just apply a one-word label to it and let it go rather than ruminating or contemplating it.

- Urge Surfing: an approach to handling cravings or impulses by imagining them as waves that will peak before slowing down and visualizing oneself as riding the wave

- Box Breathing: inhale through the nose for 4 seconds, hold it for 4 seconds, exhale through the mouth for 4 seconds, hold empty for 4 seconds; repeat

- 4-7-8 Breathing: inhale through the nose for 4 seconds, hold it for 7 seconds, and exhale through the mouth for 8 seconds; repeat

- S.T.O.P Checkpoint: Stop, Take a breath, Observe, Proceed

- Sensory Task Immersion: do a mundane task while focusing on the sensory information

Mindfulness is complemented with [[intentionality|intentionality]]; once one is mindful of one's internal patterns, one can more appropriately set their intentions to maintain or change them.
