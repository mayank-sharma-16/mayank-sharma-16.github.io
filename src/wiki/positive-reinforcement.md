# Overview
Positive reinforcement is the process through which the likelihood of a behavior is increased by adding a reward right afterward. It contrasts with [[negative-reinforcement|negative reinforcement]], in which a behavior is incentivized through removing or stopping a consequence.

Substance use disorders are initially the result of positive reinforcement, where someone's brain associates using a substance with dopamine spikes that signal it to be highly rewarding behavior. Eventually, when someone's neurochemical baselines for feeling rewarded have changed, they feel the need to use substances to feel normal. Negative reinforcement drives many people away from sobriety more than positive reinforcement drives people towards substances.
