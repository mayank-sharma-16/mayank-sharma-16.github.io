# Overview
Neurotransmitters are molecules used by neurons to transmit signals across gaps called synapses. Researchers have identified hundreds of distinct neurotransmitters. Some of the most common types include:

- Glutamate: the most common excitatory neurotransmitter, associated with learning and memory

- GABA (Gamma-aminobutyric acid): the common inhibitory neurotransmitter, associated with regulating anxiety and calming brain activity

- Dopamine: a monoamine involved in reward, motivation, and motor control

- Serotonin: a monoamine involved in regulating mood, sleep, appetite, and digestion

- Norepinephrine: a neurotransmitter that drives alertness and attention, especially as part of the body's stress response

- Acetylcholine: an excitatory neurotransmitter responsible for muscle contractions and memory

The reward circuit, or the [[mesolimbic-system|mesolimbic dopamine pathway]], issues small releases of dopamine in response to natural survival activities like eating or socializing, but addictive substances produce big floods of neurotransmitters at once. Stimulants like cocaine and amphetamines block the reuptake of dopamine or force its release. Substances like opioids inhibit the "brakes" on dopamine release by mimicking natural neurotransmitters. The brain responds to these floods in neurotransmitters by reducing its receptors available to bind to them.
