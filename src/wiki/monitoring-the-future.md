# Overview
Monitoring the Future (MTF) is an ongoing national survey that tracks substance use patterns among Americans. It has been conducted yearly since 1975 through the Survey Research Center at the University of Michigan, funded by the National Institute on Drug Abuse (NIDA). It started with 12th graders but expanded to include 8th and 10th graders in 1991. The survey follows a randomly chosen sample from each cohort of graduating students throughout their lives by checking in every two years until 30 and then every five years from 35 onward. On top of measuring drug consumption across three intervals (past 30 days, past 12 months, and lifetime usage), it also monitors perceived risks and social disapproval, educational and career aspirations, gender roles and parental influences, attitudes toward religion, political opinions, etc. MTF has often been described as a [[gold-standard|gold standard]] for tracking epidemiological changes over time.
