# Overview
Defining consciousness leads to some of the most abstract debates in philosophical discourse. For convenience, we can define consciousness as the quality of being aware of one's subjective experiences. 

Major neuroscientific schools of thought around consciousness include Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNW), Integrated Information Theory (IIT), and Attention Schema Theory. The philosophical problems include the Hard Problem, coined by David Chalmers to ask why first-person experience emerges from the brain at all, and the mind-body problem about how mental states and physical matter are related.
