# Overview
Parentification refers to the pattern of children being put into roles by their parents or guardians that are meant for adults. Instrumental parentification occurs when a child handles the cooking, cleaning, shopping, bills, raising of younger siblings, and other practical or logistical tasks. Emotional parentification occurs when a child becomes part of a parent's conflict resolution or emotional regulation strategies, such as asking a child to be a confidant, therapist, mediator, messenger, peacekeeper, etc. 

Adults who experience parentification often share some patterns in behaviors and mindsets:

- Hyper-independence

- People-pleasing

- Co-dependence

- Difficulty with boundaries

There is no official diagnosis just for parentification, but there are some contextual codes included in disease classification systems. The DSM-5 Contextual Codes allows one to note a Parent-Child Relational Problem that may affect clinical care. The ICD-11 notes a similar Caregiver-Child Relationship Problem. Psychometric researchers have proposed the Parentification Questionnaire (PQ) and the Filial Responsibility Scale (FRS) for measuring the severity of parentification and similar family dysfunction.

In legal systems, parentification may be used to describe the dynamics that fall more broadly under "neglect," "emotional abuse," or "failure to protect."
