# Overview
Neurasthenia is a diagnosis of chronic fatigue alongside distressing physical symptoms. In Western psychiatry, the diagnosis has mostly been replaced with the diagnoses of [[depression|depression]] or chronic fatigue syndrome. The World Health Organization's ICD-10 still includes it as some cultures still use it as an alternative to depression. The symptoms include:

- persistent fatigue

- headaches

- sleep disturbances or insomnia

- irritability

- inability to relax

- dizziness

- muscle aches

- gastrointestinal issues 

- impaired concentration or memory

# Historical Context
The term was popularized by George Miller Beard in 1869, and it was often attributed to living in fast-paced urban environments that exhausted mental energy. Physicians regularly prescribed stimulants like laudanum, morphine, and cocaine tonics to give people a boost, with alcohol as a widely-available depressant to counteract the energized mental state.
