# Overview
In psychology, schema refers to a pattern stored in long-term memory that's actively used to interpret information from the world. People can resist updating their schemas even in the face of clear evidence, so a common goal of therapy is helping people become aware of their schemas and change them.

The American Psychological Association offers three definitions for schema, since the concept is used commonly in different contexts within psychology and its related fields:

1. *"a collection of basic knowledge about a concept or entity that serves as a guide to perception, interpretation, imagination, or problem solving."*

2. *"a cognitive structure representing a person’s knowledge about some entity or situation, including its qualities and the relationships between these. Schemas are usually abstractions that simplify a person’s world."*

3. *"an outlook or assumption that an individual has of the self, others, or the world that endures despite objective reality. For example, “I am a damaged person” and “Anyone I trust will eventually hurt me” are negative schemas that may result from negative experiences in early childhood."*

[[early-maladaptive-schema|Early Maladaptive Schemas (EMS)]] are those schemas that were internalized early in life but have become [[maladaptive|maladaptive]] over time.
