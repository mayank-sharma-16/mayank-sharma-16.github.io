# Overview
The idea of "gateway drugs" is that someone who uses substances like [[alcohol|alcohol]], [[nicotine|nicotine]], and [[cannabis|cannabis]] will be automatically triggered into seeking out harder substances. 

However, there isn't much evidence to support the idea of gateway drugs. The idea stems from an interpretation of data that shows a correlation between those who use harder substances like [[methamphetamine|meth]] or [[heroin|heroin]] and those who used "softer" substances like alcohol or weed, where it's assumed that the softer substances cause the use of "harder" substances later in life. However, most people who use "softer" substances never end up using "harder" substances, which suggests that the "softer" substances aren't a cause but an early stage of a chronic condition that afflicts a minority of people, namely [[substance-use-disorder|Substance Use Disorder (SUD)]]. 

Gateway drugs go hand-in-hand with [[prohibitionism|prohibitionist]] and [[abstinence|abstinence-only]] approaches to substance use that advocate against any experimentation with looking to substances for relief, though [[caffeine|caffeine]] is still a notable exception in most cases.
