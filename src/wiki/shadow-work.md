# Overview
Shadow work refers to psychological practices that explore repressed or unconscious parts of the self. 

# Historical Context
The term originates with Carl Jung's work about the "shadow" that represents repressed traits. The term "shadow work" was coined by Connie Zweig and Jeremiah Abrams in 1991. Since then, the term became widely adopted in self-help and recovery circles through literature like shadow work journals that have structured prompts for self-reflection. Clinical therapy has also adopted ideas from shadow work, like in Internal Family Systems (IFS).
