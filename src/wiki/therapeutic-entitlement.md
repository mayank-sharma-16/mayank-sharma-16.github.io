# Overview
Therapeutic entitlement has a few meanings depending on the context, which usually involves power dynamics in therapy or rights to access healthcare.

# Definitions

## Power Dynamics
A study from Utrecht University by M. Guilfoyle that applies Michel Foucault's ideas about power dynamics to therapy writes:

*The very idea that a person can be understood positions him or her within an observing discourse and produces power relations and effects (Foucault, 1988). Therapy involves an intensity of observation and a relentless pursuit of meaning that is focussed exclusively on the client. A power relationship is produced in which therapists become entitled (by virtue of their expert position within therapeutic discourse) to create meaning out of the client’s conduct, and to allow this meaning to influence the interaction...*

*"they are effects of power insofar as they have been permitted expression through the therapeutic entitlement to understand. But these meanings can also, in turn, have effects of power: they can be used to structure the course and shape of the therapeutic relationship – in a way that would be intolerable in the other setting. They are used to inform therapist conduct, to generate or suppress topics of conversation, and to construct future interventions.* [^0]

## Right to Healthcare

In a 2006 paper published in the American Journal of Orthopsychiatry called *Campus Mental Health Services: Recommendations for Change*, the authors write:

*"There have been calls for discussion of mental health policy in university and college counseling centers. The concern is that shifts in the American cultural mindset have promoted the idea of 'therapeutic entitlement,' wherein students with serious mental health probles have the right to demand mental health care from the counseling center which arguably should primarily focus on the educational mission of the college."*
