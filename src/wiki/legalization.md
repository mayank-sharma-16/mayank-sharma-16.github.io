# Overview
"Legalization" in the context of drug policies refers to a framework in which some type of substance use is not only [[decriminalization|decriminalized]] but also treated as a product that can be bought, sold, and used on markets or other platforms. For instance, alcohol is a legalized drug, though it does come with age restrictions and public intoxication laws. In the United States, some states have legalized marijuana so that it can be purchased and used recreationally without a medical license or prescription from a medical practitioner. Other drugs like cocaine, heroin, and fentanyl remain criminalized. 

Proponents of legalization argue that people who use substances chronically are going to pursue their patterns regardless of whether it's illegal. In fact, we can't even reliably ensure that prisons and jails are drug-free, much less the general civilian population. This means that when drug laws are enforced, it's highly selective at the discretion of law enforcement and political agendas rather than fairly implemented as a deterrence. Instead of treating drug use as a criminal issue, legalizing it allows substances to be sold with well-regulated quality that avoids contamination and makes it easier for public health interventions to reach the populations that need it, whether that results in harm reduction or sustained abstinence.
