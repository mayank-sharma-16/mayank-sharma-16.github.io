# Overview
The Mediterranean diet refers to diets inspired by plant-heavy traditions of countries surrounding the Mediterranean Sea. Rather than strict rules, the diet emphasizes vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, and extra virgin olive oil on a day-to-day basis. Fish, poultry, eggs, daily, and cheeses should be eaten in moderate portions weekly. Red meat, refined sugars, and saturated fats like butter are to be strictly limited. The benefits from the perspective of recovery include:

- Anti-inflammatory effects: chronic substance use causes more inflammation throughout the whole body that can worsen the cravings and depression that influence unhealthy eating patterns. The Mediterranean diet tends to lead to high omega-3 fatty acids, polyphenols, and fiber which actively reduce inflammation

- Improvements to mood: the diet has been linked to a 25% to 35% reduction in the risk of developing depression and anxiety

- Gut-Brain Axis: whole foods, legumes, fiber, and fermented foods promote healthy gut bacteria that are linked to the regulation of neurotransmitters like serotonin and dopamine.

- Cardiovascular health: the diet is associated with lowering bad cholesterol, managing blood pressure, and reducing the risk of heart attacks and strokes by as much as 30%. It's also associated with lower instances of type 2 diabetes and other chronic diseases.
