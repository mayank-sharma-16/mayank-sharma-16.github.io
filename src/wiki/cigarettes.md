# Overview
Cigarettes are rolls of paper containing finely cut [[tobacco|tobacco]] that are lit with a flame on one end so that smoke can be inhaled from the other end. They are considered [[tobacco-containing-tobacco-products|tobacco containing tobacco products (TCTP)]], likely the first one that comes to mind for most people.

Modern cigarettes include about 600 additives to raw tobacco leaf. Freebase [[nicotine|nicotine]]: ammonia is added to nicotine so that it vaporizes more efficiently and crosses the blood-brain barrier faster. Sensory modifiers like sugars, cocoa, and menthol are added to ease the naturally harsh character of burning tobacco. 

Upon being lit, cigarettes produce a mixture of over 7000 chemicals like monoxide, [[formaldehyde|formaldehyde]], arsenic, and at least 69 [[carcinogen|carcinogens]]. Cigarette smoke is recognized as a leading cause of disease and death since it damages nearly every organ system.
