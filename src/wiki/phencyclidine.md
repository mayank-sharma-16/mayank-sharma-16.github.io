# Overview
Phencyclidine, also called angel dust or PCP, is an addictive drug that causes dissociation, euphoria, mood swings, hallucinations, and paranoia. It was originally used as an anesthetic until it was noted to have severe side effects that persisted after operations. It's now classified as a Schedule II controlled substance in the United States, though illicit recreational use continues; PCP can be snorted, swallowed, injected, or smoked, sometimes by dipping products like cigarettes or joints in them.

## Short-Term Effects
The short-term effects of PCP include:

- Dissociation

- Sensory Distortions

- Ataxia (loss of coordination)

- Hypertension (elevated blood pressure)

- Tachycardia (increased heart rate)

- Shallow Breathing

- Sweating

- Nystagmus (rapid and involuntary eye movements)

- Delirium

- [[Paranoia|Paranoia]]

- Hallucinations

- Agitation

- [[analgesia|Analgesic]] painkilling

- Muscle rigidity

- Seizures

- Catatonia

- Hyperthermia (increase in body temperature)

- Respiratory Depression

- Coma

- Death

## Long-Term Effects

- Memory Loss

- Stuttering

- Slurred Speech

- Difficulty Concentrating

- Persistent Psychosis

- Depression

- Anxiety

- Suicidal Ideation

- Toxic Encephalopathy

- Kidney Damage
