# Overview
A motivated belief is a belief that someone holds because they want it to be true rather than because they think the evidence suggests the belief is most likely true. Another term for this phenomenon is "motivated reasoning." 

In psychology, it's considered a self-defense mechanism since someone's justification can be logical or rational while still being motivated by a personal reason. This helps someone feel better or more secure in their worldview, but it can also be a way to combat feelings of [[learned-helplessness|learned helplessness]], where someone might overestimate their chances of success to stay persistent. This is especially interesting given the phenomenon of depressive realism, in which individuals suffering from depression or learned helplessness can be more accurate at judging their lack of control.
