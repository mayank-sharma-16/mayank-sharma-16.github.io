# Overview
Medicalization is the process in which problems and concepts become defined as medical conditions to be treated, usually involving clinical and research institutions.

The history of policy around drugs and treatment for substance use disorder is loaded with moralization that doesn't account for the way chronic substance use produces physiological changes in the brain. Although those like Benjamin Rush advocated for treating alcoholism as an "odious disease" as far back as 1784, the medicalization of alcoholism began in earnest in the 20th century. Alcoholics Anonymous (AA) was founded in 1935 and was associated with the [[a-disease-like-any-other|"disease like any other"]] campaigns that framed alcoholism as a kind of allergy. In 1956, the American Medical Association (AMA) officially declared alcoholism as an illness and began shifting the focus of addiction treatment from peer-led spiritual groups to methods pioneered from psychology, psychiatry, and neuroscience.
