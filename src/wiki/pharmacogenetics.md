# Overview
Pharmacogenetics is the study of how a person's genes influence the way a drug is metabolized. It's considered a more narrow term than [[pharmacogenomics|pharmacogenomics]], but both are often used interchangeably.

For any particular substance consumed, someone might be considered a poor metabolizer, an intermediate metabolizer, or an ultra-rapid metabolizer. Poor metabolizers might break down a drug too slowly and suffer from side effects because it lingers in higher levels in their system. Ultra-rapid metabolizers might break down a drug too quickly and limit its effectiveness. Genetic testing can reveal whether someone has predispositions that might make a particular medication more or less effective. This is true across antidepressants, pain medications, blood thinners, antiplatelet drugs, cancer drugs, antivirals, antibiotics, and allergic reaction.
