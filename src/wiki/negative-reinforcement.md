# Overview
Negative reinforcement is the process through which the likelihood of a behavior is influenced through the incentive of avoiding or removing a stimulus. Note that "negative" doesn't mean bad or unpleasant here; it means removing or negating some effect.

In the context of substance use, [[positive-reinforcement|positive reinforcement]] is usually the initial motivation since consuming the substance produces pleasure or a high. Eventually, as someone becomes dependent or addicted to the substance to the point of withdrawals without it, negative reinforcement fuels substance use since one's baseline state becomes seemingly intolerable and they would use to negate that feeling. One of the focuses of modern addiction treatment involves building coping skills that include [[distress-tolerance|distress tolerance]] which can help interrupt the cycles of negative reinforcement, since cravings and withdrawals are temporary experiences that will go away if tolerated.
