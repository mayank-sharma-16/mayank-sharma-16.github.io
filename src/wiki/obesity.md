# Overview
Obesity is a chronic disease characterized by carrying excessive fat to the point of experiencing health issues. 

Much like substance use, overeating foods that are high in fat, sugar, or salt triggers a release of dopamine in the [[mesolimbic-system|mesolimbic reward pathway]]. Chronically overeating these foods can build tolerance as well since the brain will reduce its dopamine receptors in response to the spikes from eating. However, in the same way that recreational substance use can be harmful even without a dependence or addiction, obesity can be harmful even if someone doesn't meet the criteria for a food addiction. Up to 70% of someone's Body-Mass Index (BMI) is determined by genetics, so it's possible for many people to develop obesity as the result of a sedentary lifestyle or mild overconsumption if they're genetically predisposed to it. For many who are in recovery from substance use, high-sugar and high-fat comfort foods can become a new addiction that transfers from their drugs of choice. Research tends to find that BMI and obesity are higher in patients who are in residential alcohol or drug treatment programs than the general population.
