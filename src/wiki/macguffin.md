# Overview
A "MacGuffin" describes a plot device that is mostly irrelevant to the actions and events of the story except for giving the characters an initial motivation. 

Substances and process [[addiction|addictions]] alike are often MacGuffins in stories, especially as a result of anti-drug movements that produce lots of substance use depictions in the context of crime and underground subcultures. Some of these tropes include:

- drug shipments as a highly valuable item that characters chase or fight over, giving law enforcement and criminal groups reasons to have conflicts with each other

- dealing drugs as an easy way of making lots of money for characters who might be otherwise ordinary, setting up characters to be independently wealthy and free from daily obligations

- [[active-addiction|active addiction]] as a reason that a character might do something irrational or extreme, whether it's from the desperation to keep affording the addiction or from an altered and excited mental state 

- [[overdose|overdoses]] as tragic deaths that allow any number of family members to be killed off while invoking a sense of prolonged struggle or suffering 

# Examples

- [[bad-boys|Bad Boys (1995)]]: though the movie involves $100 million in stolen [[heroin|heroin]], the movie is really about providing the protagonists a reason to engage in buddy-cop tropes involving action scenes and crass banter

- [[breaking-bad|Breaking Bad]]: Walter White's [[methamphetamine|meth]] could be changed with almost any other illicit substance and have the same outcome since the show centers on how Walt's ego interacts with personalities deep in the drug trade and criminal underworld

- Dune: Spice is a stand-in for oil and drugs as a substance that various factions use to enhance their power and prosperity, towards which their political games are played
