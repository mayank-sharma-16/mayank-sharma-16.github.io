# Overview
Kombucha is a [[fermentation|fermented]] beverage produced by a Symbiotic Culture of Bacteria and Yeast (SCOBY) that's added to sweetened teas. Yeast breaks down the sugars into ethanol, and then bacteria consumes that to produce organic acids that are responsible for fizzy, vinegary flavors. In theory, though kombucha will contain trace amounts of alcohol, the concentration in commercially sold kombucha leads it to being nonalcoholic. However, kombucha that isn't refrigerated can continue to ferment and raise the alcohol content, so it's a good idea to skip the drink altogether if avoiding alcohol is an important concern of yours. This is especially true for kombucha that's been made at home without industrial equipment to monitor the concentration of alcohol or maintain the proper temperature storage conditions.
