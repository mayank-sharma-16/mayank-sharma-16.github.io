# Overview
Permanent Supportive Housing (PSH) is a housing intervention that follows a Housing-First to provide someone with a permanent lease without preconditions like treatment compliance. The target demographic includes chronically homeless individuals or households with a disability, serious mental illness, or chronic health condition. 

PSH programs are one of the most effective ways to address homelessness, as up to 85% of chronically homeless individuals remain housed after one year following a PSH program. Individuals in PSH are also more likely to reduce their substance use voluntarily and participate in outpatient care for mental health. It also provides a stable place for treatment providers to reach an individual and seek care before a crisis happens, which studies show has led to a 40% reduction in visits to emergency departments, psychiatric hospitals, and shelters. It also decreases interactions with law enforcement, with a decrease in jail stays and citations.
