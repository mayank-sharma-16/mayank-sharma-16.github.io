# Overview
Radical self-acceptance is the acknowledgement and embrace of your entire self with all your experiences without judging yourself or denying reality. 

# Historical Context
Radical self-acceptance emerged after the ideas of unconditional positive regard in an era that focused on the pathologies caused by the repressed unconscious. Marsha Linehan, a Zen practitioner as well as a psychologist, introduced the idea of radical acceptance to [[dialectical-behavioral-therapy|Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT)]], in which someone accepts reality as it is without judging it or fighting it. That includes acknowledging that pain is a part of life and must be endured, which makes it a relevant tool when building distress tolerance skills and capacities. In 2003, Tara Brach derived "radical self-acceptance" from Linehan's "radical acceptance" in order to describe a complete acceptance of oneself to remedy the feeling that one is fundamentally broken or flawed.
