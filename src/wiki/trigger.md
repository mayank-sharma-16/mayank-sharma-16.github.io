# Overview
In psychology, a trigger is any stimulus, internal or external, that activates a strong reaction based in a prior [[trauma|traumatic]] event. 

# Models

## Diathesis-Stress Model
The Diathesis-Stress Model, also called Vulnerability Stress Model, proposes that a condition is triggered when an underlying vulnerability (diathesis) intersects with environmental factors (stressors). ## ABC Model (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy)
Within [[cognitive-behavioral-therapy|Cognitive Behavioral Therapy]], the ABC Model tracks the emotional process initiated by triggers: Activating Event, Beliefs, Consequences.

## Dual-Representation Theory
This model takes a neurocognitive approach and states that trauma is stored in two memory systems: Verbally Accessible Memories (VAM) and Situationally Accessible Memories (SAM). VAM are the conscious narratives processed by the brain's hippocampus with chronological cause-and-effect. SAM are processed by the amygdala and include sensory and emotional experiences. Triggers force the brain to replay SAM without the context of time, which leads to re-experiencing parts or the whole of the trauma as if it is happening now. Some of these episodes of re-experiencing are called [[flashbacks|flashbacks]] or [[emotional-flashbacks|emotional flashbacks]].

## ABC Method (Applied Behavioral Analysis)
The ABC Method is an alternative framing to the ABC Model, though both accomplish a similar breakdown: Antecedent, Behavior, Consequence.
