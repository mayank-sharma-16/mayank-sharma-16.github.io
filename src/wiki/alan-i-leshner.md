# Overview
Alan Leshner is a psychologist who served as director of the National Institute on Drug Abuse (NIDA) and the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH).

# Education

- Bachelor of Arts (A.B.) in Psychology, Franklin and Marshall College (1965): graduated with honors

- Master of Science (M.S.) in Physiological Psychology, Rutgers University (1967)

- Doctor of Philosophy (Ph.D.) in Physiological Psychology, Rutgers University (1969)

# Affiliations

- Bucknell University (1969-1979): taught and researched psychology and behavioral endocrinology

- Wisconsin Regional Primate Research Center

- Budapest Postgraduate Medical School

- Weizmann Institute of Science 

- [[national-science-foundation|National Science Foundation]] (1979-1988)

- National Institute of Mental Health (1988-1994)

- [[national-institute-on-drug-abuse|National Institute on Drug Abuse]] (1994-2001)

- [[american-association-for-the-advancement-of-science|American Association for the Advancement of Science]] (2001-2015)

- National Science Board (2004-2016)

## Papers

- [Addiction is a brain disease, and it matters (1997)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9311924/)

*"Scientific advances over the past 20 years have shown that drug addiction is a chronic, relapsing disease that results from the prolonged effects of drugs on the brain... Recognizing addiction as a chronic, relapsing brain disorder characterized by compulsive drug seeking and use can impact society's overall health and social policy strategies and help diminish the health and social costs associated with drug abuse and addiction."*
