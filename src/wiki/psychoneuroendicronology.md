# Overview
Psychoneuroendocrinology is the study of the interactions between mental processes, the nervous system, and the endocrine system that regulates hormones.

[[allostasis|Allostasis]] is the process through which the body adjusts itself to maintain stability. [[allostatic-load|Allostatic load]] refers to the impacts on the body over time when stress responses are chronically triggered, which involves the endocrine system releasing stress hormones for a cascade of bodily reactions.

Three major axes of the neuroendocrine system that intersect with psychology include the:

- Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) Axis: the feedback loops for stress, trauma, and depression, with cortisol being especially significant 

- Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Gonadal (HPG) Axis: the circuits for mood disorders, aggression, and reproductive behaviors driven by sex hormones (testosterone, estrogen, and progesterone)

- Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Thyroid (HPT) Axis: the axis for metabolic speed and the thyroid hormones that influence mood, energy, and cognition
