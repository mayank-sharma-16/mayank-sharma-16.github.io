# Overview
A saloon is an older term for a bar or tavern where people socialize while drinking and watching entertainment. 

# Historical Context
In America, saloons emerged in the early 19th century to serve traders, miners, and cattle ranchers in small towns, and they were mostly designed to serve men. They became important local institutions that also acted as community centers, post offices, and voting stations. Saloons even gained political power themselves and lobbied for favorable regulations and policies. The [[temperance|temperance]] movement that brought on [[prohibitionism|Prohibition]] in the early 20th century led to the widespread destruction of saloon culture in America. Sometimes, it also meant the literal destruction of saloons. Activists like Carry Nation conducted "hatchetations" during the movement for Prohibition, which meant tearing down saloons with hatches.
