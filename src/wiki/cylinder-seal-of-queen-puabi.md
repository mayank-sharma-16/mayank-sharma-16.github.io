# Overview
The Cylinder Seal of Queen Puabi is an lapis lazuli seal that was found in the Royal Cemetery at Ur in the queen's tomb with her body in 1928. It holds a cuneiform inscription of her name without any mention of male family members, which would be uncommon for female royalty of that era. It also depicts an upper register featuring Puabi raising a cup alongside two male guests while being served by attendants and a lower register with two groups facing each other across a table piled with food as attendants pour drinks.

Queen Puabi ruled Early Dynastic Sumer, where drinking at royal banquets had social and religious significance in enhancing ritual feasts, cementing diplomatic alliances, and demonstrating the luxury of noble classes. Sumerian religion includes the goddess Ninkasi, associated with beer and [[alcohol|alcohol]], and it was thought that drinking alcohol lowered the barrier between oneself and sacred planes. Brewing alcohol and running taverns were considered female professions (sabitu), so the association between alcohol and women's roles was strong. Puabi's depiction in the seal thus commemorates her as an intersection between spirituality and femininity, facilitating both the creation and serving of alcohol through her royal influence.
