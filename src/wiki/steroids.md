# Overview
In chemistry, steroids are a class of organic compounds necessary for the body's functions. The two main categories include corticosteroids and anabolic-androgenic steroids.

Corticosteroids, also called glucocorticoids, are hormones similar to cortisol that manage stress response, immune response, and inflammation. They're prescribed as pills, syrups, inhalers, nasal sprays, skin creams, and injections in order to address conditions resulting from inflammation or overactive immune system responses. That includes asthma, arthritis, lupus, inflammatory bowel disease, and allergies. Side effects involve high blood sugar, weight gain, insomnia, osteoporosis, weakened immune system, and skin thinning.

Anabolic-androgenic steroids are variants of testosterone whose medical uses involve delaying puberty or addressing muscle-wasting diseases. These are the steroids that are used by those attempting to enhance their workouts for more muscle or faster gains, often in doses that are up to 100 times higher than those in medical treatments. Side effects include high blood pressure, elevated LDL cholesterol, lowered HDL cholesterol, thickening of heart muscles, increased risk of heart attacks and strokes, testicular shrinkage, reduced sperm count, infertility, deepening voice, facial hair growth, menstrual irregularities, stunted height, liver damage, tumors, severe mood swings, mania, and clinical depression.
