# Overview
Auguste-Henri Forel was a professor of psychiatry at the University of Zurich and co-formulated the [[neuron|neuron]] theory in 1887. He served as the director of the university's Burgholzli asylum from 1879 to 1898 and researched the potential applications of hypnotism. He also researched ants and published works like The Social Life of Ants.

In 1889, he partnered with Jakob Bosshard to open a specialized treatment facility for [[substance-use-disorder|substance use disorders (SUDs)]] in Ellikon an der Thur, which now operates as the Forel Clinic. Although Forel opposed [[alcohol|alcohol]] on the basis of it being a [[neurotoxin|neurotoxin]] that damaged brain tissue and examined alcohol use as a medical issue rather than a moral failing, he also adopted [[eugenics|eugenics]] perspectives that argued alcohol consumption directly passed on harm to one's future generations. He campaigned against alcohol use through lecturing and organizing with [[temperance|temperance]] movements across North America and Europe, connecting it to mental illness, poverty, crime, and domestic violence.

Forel was a close associate of [[emil-kraepelin|Emil Kraepelin]], with whom he founded the Association of Abstinent Doctors in 1896 under the belief that psychiatrists ought to be fully [[abstinence|abstinent]], since any amount of alcohol consumption has harms and moderate use was argued to be an illusion.
