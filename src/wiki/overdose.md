# Overview
An overdose refers to the idea of taking too much of something, resulting in negative consequences. 

However, many overdoses can be caused as the result of using multiple substances at once whose combinded effects compound into negative consequences. For instance, mixing opioids with alcohol can lead to a stronger respiratory depression than one might expect from having used each alone, leading to the same thing as an overdose on just one substance. Substances don't just add up in their effects, they can multiply to cause a reaction many times stronger than each individual substance might provoke. 

[[harm-reduction|Harm reduction]] practices like [[overdose-prevention-site-ops|Overdose Prevention Sites (OPS)]] and [[opioid-treatment-program|Opioid Treatment Programs (OTPs)]] often measure the impact of their success by the number of overdoses prevented or reversed.
