# Overview
The National Health Service (NHS) is the healthcare system of the United Kingdom, which uses public funds to provide most services for free at the point of use for legal residents.

Most care for [[substance-use-disorder|Substance Use Disorders (SUDs)]] comes from Community Drug and Alcohol Teams (CDATs), where a [[case-management|keyworker]] coordinates [[detoxification|detoxification]], medications like buprenorphone or methadone for [[opioid-use-disorder|Opioid Use Disorder (OUD)]], [[cognitive-behavioral-therapy|Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (CBT)]], motivational interviewing, etc. For severe cases, the NHS uses Inpatient Detoxification Units (IPUs), where patients at-risk of fatal [[withdrawal|withdrawals]] will be admitted to a hospital ward for 24-hour monitoring. Residential treatment program placement is funded by local councils or charities rather than the NHS. 

The NHS also manages [[harm-reduction|harm-reduction]] initiatives like [[syringe-services-program|Needle and Syringe Programmes (NSPs)]], [[naloxone|Naloxone (Narcan)]] distribution, and rapid testing for [[sexually-transmitted-infections|Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)]]. 

NHS drug and alcohol services generally accept self-referrals, so a previous appointment with a [[primary-care-physician|General Practitioner]] that leads to a referral isn't necessary. The NHS Find Local Addiction Services Tool allows users to search for services by postcode to find clinics in a desired location. The NHS-backed Talk to Frank platform provides a 24/7 confidential helpline and database of support services across England.
