# Overview
Mandatory minimum sentencing requirements are laws that require judges to impose a minimum sentence for certain crimes without considering the specific circumstances of the case. Together with [[mass-incarceration|mass incarceration]], it forms a core part of the [[war-on-drugs|War on Drugs]] in the United States.

Consider a wealthy defendant who commits corporate fraud that wipes out the retirement savings of thousands of people. They might be able to afford a strong legal team who could find a sympathetic judge and hire public relations firms to improve their presentation in court. They can also use their positions to make trades with prosecutors involving financial data that plays a factor in other cases. It's also possible for an individual judge to be compromised in some way with a conflict of interest that incentivizes lenient sentencing. A mandatory minimum on fraud over some amount might ensure the defendant can't use their wealth to mitigate the consequences of their crimes.

Now consider a low-income defendant who took money to deliver illicit drugs and has no prior criminal history, like a college student who wanted easy money or a part-time worker who was behind on rent. Sentencing someone like that to a decade or more of time in prison because of a mandatory minimum on drug trafficking charges can seem disproportionately harsh. However, the judge has no ability to lower the severity of the punishment in response to the specific circumstances of the case.
