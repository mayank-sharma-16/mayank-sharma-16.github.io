# Overview
The National Center for Advancing Translational Sciences (NCATS) is a part of the U.S. National Institutes of Health (NIH) and has the goal of translating scientific discoveries into new treatments to be delivered to patients. Their projects include Clinical and Translational Science Awards (CTSA) Program that supports medical research institutions doing translational research with clinics, provides resources to advocacy groups like the "NCATS Toolkit for Patient-Focused Therapy Development," hosts an [[assay|assay]] guidance manual for early-stage drug discovery, and advances New Approach Methodologies (NAMs).

Unlike other NIH institutes that focus on behavioral therapy as part of the Helping to End Addiction Long-term (HEAL) Initiative, NCATS focuses on anti-[[addiction|addiction]] and pain management therapies that come out of laboratory research. They've designed 3-D human tissue chips to mimic human brain systems since animal testing fails often in predicting how medications might work in the brain, curated the HEAL Target and Compound Library featuring nearly 3000 molecules, and sped up clinical trials through its CTSA Trial Innovation Network.
