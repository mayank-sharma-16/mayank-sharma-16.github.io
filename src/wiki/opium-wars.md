# Overview
The Opium Wars were two wars between 1839-1842 and 1856-1860, with the Chinese Qing dynasty on one side and Great Britan and France on the other. Western nations had a high demand for Chinese products like tea and silk, which China demanded payment for in silver instead of trading for Western manufactured goods. The British East India Company made up the deficit by trading opium grown in India to black market merchants in China. This led to an opioid epidemic that was tearing apart daily life in China for millions, so the government cracked down on opium by seizing and destroying stashes when they were found. This included the destruction of the property of British entities, who wanted to retaliate with the goal of forcing China to open its market and abide by Western standards of international trade and law. This eventually escalated into military conflict.

The wars still hold significance today for many in China, or the international diaspora, as a clear marker of the beginning of Western imperialism in East Asia.
