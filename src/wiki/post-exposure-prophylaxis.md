# Overview
Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is a treatment started after exposure to an infection, most commonly in the context of HIV, Hepatitis B, and bacterial STIs.

Populations that struggle with substance use disorders (SUDs) might engage in risky behaviors like sharing needles, syringes, and water or having unprotected sex. PEP can be delivered within 72 hours of someone's exposure to one of these risk factors. For bacterial STIs, a single dose of Doxycycline STI Postexposure Prophylaxis (Doxy PEP) suffices. For HIV, a 28-day course of antiretrovirals is needed.

If someone is routinely exposed to the risks mentioned above rather than as an isolated emergency event, they might transition to [[pre-exposure-prophylaxis|Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)]] right after their PEP dose completes. PrEP is recognized as a highly effective, evidence-backed [[harm-reduction|harm reduction]] tool.
