# Overview
People-pleasing is a pattern where someone prioritizes the desires and emotional states of others over their own, usually as a result of fearing conflict or rejection. This isn't the same as kindness or empathy though. People-pleasing instincts are often a coping mechanism or [[fight-flight-freeze-fawn|trauma response like fawning]] driven by a need to have a stable environment or to secure external approval. The term is not a formal diagnosis in the DSM-5, although Dependent Personality Disorder (DPD) describes patterns of people-pleasing in its criteria of symptoms. 

Within recovery circles, people-pleasing is one of the most common patterns that gets addressed, especially when it involves waiving boundaries in the short-term that comes out as resentment in the long-term or codependent relationships with people whose behavior presents risks to sobriety.
