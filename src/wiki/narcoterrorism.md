# Overview
Narcoterrorism refers to the bidirectional relationship between drug trafficking and terrorism: traffickers use terrorist tactics to expand and protect their operations, and terrorists participate in trafficking to fund their operations.

The [[drug-enforcement-agency|Drug Enforcement Agency (DEA)]] defines it more narrowly in the direction of funding terrorism through some relationship with the illicit drug trade:

*"a subset of terrorism, in which terrorist groups, or associated individuals, participate directly or  indirectly in the cultivation, manufacture, transportation, or  distribution of controlled substances and the monies derived from these  activities. Further, narco-terrorism may be characterized by the  participation of groups or associated individuals in taxing, providing  security for, or otherwise aiding or abetting drug trafficking endeavors in an effort to further, or fund, terrorist activities."* [^0]

# Historical Context
The term was first popularized in 1983 by then-President Fernando Belaunde Terry of Peru when describing violence by drug traffickers directed towards anti-narcotics law enforcement.
