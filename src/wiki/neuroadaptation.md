# Overview
Neuroadaptation describes the process through which the brain changes its structure and function in response to internal or external conditions.

Within the context of substance use, the brain's neuroadaptation is what leads to dependence and cravings. When substances repeatedly cause massive spikes in neurotransmmitters in the nucleus accumbens, which holds the reward center, the brain reduces the number of receptors for those neurotransmitters and slows down their natural production. As applied to dopmaine, the brain's threshold for feeling the pleasure becomes too high to feel rewarded from ordinary activities like eating or socializing, which then drives cravings to use. Over sustained chronic use, this also leads to greater tolerance for the substance.
