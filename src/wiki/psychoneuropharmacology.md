# Overview
Psychoneuropharmacology (alternatively, neuropsychopharmacology), is a study of the way chemical reactions alter the brain's functions and mental processes. It's a specific subset of [[psychopharmacology|psychopharmacology]].

Medications for the central nervous system (CNS) used to be organized by the Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) classification system proposed by the World Health Organization (WHO), but a major shift began in 2014 through the Neuroscience-based Nomenclature (NbN) proposed by the European College of Neuropsychopharmacology's (ECNP) Nomenclature Task Force. Clinical practitioners and bureaucratic systems might still be using ATC due to the way it was built into early medical technology systems, but scientific standards recommend NbN systems.
