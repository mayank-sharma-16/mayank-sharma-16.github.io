# Overview
The Washingtonian Temperance Society was founded in 1840 by six recovering alcoholics who practiced [[abstinence|abstinence]] pledges, delivered "experience speeches" about their past, and actively recruited drunk men in their community. Their structure was focused on [[mutual-aid|mutual aid]] and public [[confession|confessions]] while generally minimizing the role of sectarian religious and politics in their meetings.

At one point, more than a tenth of Baltimore's population and seven percent of New York City's population were part of the Washington Temperance Society, or the Washingtonians. By 1845, most of the societies had collapsed and become inactive. Alcoholics Anonymous (AA) co-founder William Wilson (Bill W.) explicitly notes that the Washingtonians were an early mutual aid group for struggling alcoholics that fell apart because their involvement in outside issues drove them away from a primary purpose. Tradition Ten of AA is a rule derived from that historical lesson.
