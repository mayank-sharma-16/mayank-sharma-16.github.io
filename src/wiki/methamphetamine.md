# Overview
Methamphetamine is a synthetic stimulant derived from amphetamines with a methyl group added and is classified as a Schedule II controlled substance. The methyl group makes methamphetamine more soluble in fat than standard amphetamine, which also allows it to cross the blood-brain barrier faster. It's usually consumed as either powder, pills, or rocks in a form called crystal meth. In the short-term, the effects include:

- Euphoria, or extreme sensations of pleasure or feelings of confidence

- Hyperactivity, as increased physical activity and talkativeness

- Cardiovascular stress, such as elevated heart rate and blood pressure

- Hyperthermia, a rise in body temperature that can strain organs or lead to organ failure

- Appetite suppression

- Insomnia

- Rapid, shallow breathing

In the long-term, the effects include:

- Impaired memory, decision-making, and motor skills

- Paranoia, hallucinations, and persistent delusions

- Dental decay from dry mouth, poor hygiene, and teeth grinding

- Sores or wounds resulting from scratching or picking skin, sometimes resulting in infections

- Strokes, heart attacks, and cardiomyopathy

- Anhedonia

- Malnutrition and muscle wasting
