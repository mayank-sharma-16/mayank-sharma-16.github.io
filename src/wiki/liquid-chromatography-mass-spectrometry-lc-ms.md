# Overview
Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) is a technique for detecting molecules by their structure and metabolites that's considered far more reliable than testing with [[assay|immunoassays]], which examine antibodies to identify general chemical families. Its sensitivity means that it can detect very small traces of a chemical in biological samples, which also means it can detect evidence of drug ingestion further back in time. 

If you've ever heard of over-the-counter medications accidentally triggering a false positive for amphetamines or similar situations, it's because the immunoassays react to the OTC having similar structures as the substance indicated by the false positive, even if the actual bodily responses are completely different. LC-MS separates compounds by mass with high precision through an analysis of weight and fragmentation patterns. This also gives LC-MS tests the advantage of producing data that can be revisited to scan for a new molecule whereas immunoassays require new, specific antibody tests for each new compound.

# Commentary

If you test positive for a substance based on an immunoassay, you should know that these are presumptive screenings and not definitive evidence. If you're facing any kind of consequence as the result of testing positive, you can try to contest it by having it sent to an outside lab for confirmation through LC-MS/MS or gas chromatograpy-mass spectrometry (GC-MS). For instance, some CBD products may contain trace amounts of THC that trigger false positives for immunoassays, but an LC-MS/MS or GC-MS can identify the THC as below a legal threshold. You may also have heard of cough syrup or other over-the-counter medications triggering false positive for amphetamines.
