# Overview
A Quality-Adjusted Life Year (QALY) is a health-economics metrics that combines the length of life and quality of life to measure the value of some intervention.

A 1.0 QALY represents one year lived in perfect health. 0.0 represents death. Negative scores can result if someone enters a state considered worse than death. It allows for rough calculations when making decisions in environments with scarce resources. For instance, someone might live a year in perfect health (1 QALY) without a surgical intervention, or they might live two years with a 0.75 quality-of-life score with the intervention. 2 x .75 = 1.5, so according to QALY, the greater value is found in doing the surgical intervention. Obviously, individual preferences may differ enormously. The QALY unit just allows policymakers and administrators to look at the relative effectiveness or rough outcomes of a set of decision-making criteria.
