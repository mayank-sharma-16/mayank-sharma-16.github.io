# Overview
In psychology, splitting is a defense mechanism in which someone views somethings in black-and-white terms: either they're entirely good or entirely bad. It protects the ego from experiencing the distress of [[ambivalence|ambivalence]], and it's commonly associated with Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD). 

For instance, someone might have a pleasant dream that leaves them with an "all-good" version of their partner in mind when they wake up: "they're my favorite sight to see when I wake up." If they then realize their partner hasn't done the dishes as promised, they might quickly switch to an "all-bad" version: "they're so irresponsible, it's immature and disgusting." The ambivalent state of "I love my partner but I feel irritated when they forget to do what they promised." is too difficult to hold. The same person might then switch to the "all-good" version if they fear that their partner might abandon them, and they might switch back to the "all-bad" version of their partner sets an actual boundary that makes distance that hurts to accept.
