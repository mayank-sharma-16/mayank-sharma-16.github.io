# Overview
The American Heart Association (AHA) is a nonprofit dedicated to reducing the effects of cardiovascular disease and stroke through funding research, educating the public on healthy living, and standardizing accessible cardiac care. [^0]

Research published by the AHA shows that deaths from heart disease directly related to substance use have risen by an average of 4% per year between 1999 and 2019. [^1] They recommend against drinking alcohol for any potential benefits, with heavy drinking being a leading cause of hypertension, stroke, arrhythmias, and alcoholic cardiomyopathy. They've also called cocaine "the perfect heart attack drug" because it spikes blood pressure, influences the heart's electrical system, and causes fatal arrhythmias by increasing oxygen demand while constricting blood vessels. [[intravenous|Intravenous (IV)]] drug use can also introduce infections into the body that damage heart valves. The AHA actively advocates for equal treatment of those who struggle with substance use disorders, as many providers will discriminate against those with a history of substance use and even deny care. With the increasing concern in recent decades about opioid [[overdose|overdoses]], the AHA educates the public and healthcare workforce about administering [[naloxone|Naloxone (Narcan)]] in between cycles of CPR.
