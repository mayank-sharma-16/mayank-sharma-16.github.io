# Overview
7 Cups is a non-clinical emotional support platform driven by volunteer listeners and [[peer-recovery|peer support]] those struggling with [[substance-use-disorder|Substance Use Disorder (SUD)]] and other mental health disorders. [^0] 

The platform provides:

- Addiction Chat Room: real-time peer encouragement, discussion about [[relapse-prevention|relapse prevention]], and strategies for developing [[coping-skills|coping skills]] [^1]

- Community Sub-Forums: topic-specific threads within [[addiction|addiction]] and [[recovery|recovery]], daily check-ins, and peer accountability [^2]

- Self-Help Support Guides: simple explanations for mental health disorders like [[attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder|ADHD]], therapeutic modalities like [[acceptance-and-commitment-therapy|ACT]], and common traumas like [[domestic-violence|domestic violence]] [^3]

Since 2024, the company has been the focus of public scrutiny due to safety concerns involving their moderation quality and unethically building profiles of therapists using public information.

# Historical Context

- 2013: the company was founded by Glen Moriarty in 2013 as 7 Cups of Tea with backing from Y Combinator. It was named after a 9th-century poem by Lu Tong that described the pleasures and states of mind that accompany each cup of tea in a long drinking session, with transcendence and enlightenment as otherworldly experiences by the end. In its first year, it raised about $2.1 million in seed funding from YC, Andreessen Horowitz, and General Catalyst while partnering with Tumblr as a crisis-routing service. 

- 2015: a study by Amit Baumel that was published in the [[journal-of-mental-health|Journal of Mental Health]] found "high user satisfaction with the support provided by 7COT listeners and, on average, users who indicated to receive psychotherapy in their past marked the listeners' support to be as helpful as psychotherapy... receiving support from volunteers makes users feel that the support is more genuine"

- 2016: the company rebranded into "7 Cups" from "7 Cups of Tea" and won the [[stanford-medicine-x-prize-for-health-care-systems-design|Stanford Medicine X Prize for Health Care Systems Design]] [^5]. At this point, they reported 160,000 listeners across 189 countries and 140 languages helping 800,000 people a month.

- 2018: the platform began including paid subscription services to message with licensed therapists and partnered with major institutions, like universities and [[national-alliance-on-mental-illness|NAMI]]. Studies in JMIR Mental Health and Internet Interventions evaluates 7 Cups as a tool for addressing perinatal depression and disorders on the [[schizophrenia|schizophrenia]] spectrum.

- 2024: 7Cups claims to have reached over 73 million people in 190 countries with 570,000 trained volunteers and over 2 billion messages. Moriarty wins the [[country-leadership-award|Country Leadership Award]] from the eMental Health International Collaborative. In the same year, Mashable published a five-part investigative series on moderation failures that made the platform unsafe for minors and those in crisis situations, leading the state of California to cancel a contract with 7 Cups to provide behavioral health support for youths. [^7]

- 2025: 7 Cups faced warnings from social work associations and a class-action lawsuit for scraping public registries to build profiles of mental health professionals without their consent

- 2026: Choosing Therapy, an independent consumer watchdog, issued strong advisories against 7 Cups for ongoing concerns about moderation quality and a lack of compliance with clinical care standards due to out-of-state and out-of-country counselors [^8]
