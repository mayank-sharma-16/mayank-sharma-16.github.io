# Overview
The Mental Status Examination (MSE) is a long-standing psychiatric assessment that a clinician conducts through semi-structured conversation as a screening for someone's mental state. The kind of observations a clinician makes generally falls into one of the follow categories:

- Appearance: age, weight, appropriate clothing, grooming, unique skin marks or tattoos, posture, balance, eye contact

- Behavior: hyperactivity, agitation, catatonia, restlessness, tremors, tics, jaw movement, slowed movement, notable postures, twitches

- Speech: speed, fluency, tone, quality, volume, and unusual language

- Attitude: cooperative, positive, negative

- Mood: client self-rating and self-reporting

- Affect: quality, range, stability, appropriateness, congruence, consistency

- Cognition: level of consciousness, short-term memory, long-term memory, recall, reasoning, calculation, alertness

- Insight: awareness of the problem and symptoms, ability to describe the timeline and causes of the problem, ability to articulate how they're feeling and functioning

- Judgment: level of impulse control, adaptability in decision-making, participation in their own care

- Concentration: attentiveness, drowsiness, distraction

- Orientation: knowledge of personal identity, where one is, what the date is, etc.

- Thought Process: circumstantial, tangential, loose associations, echolalia, disorganization

- Thought Content: delusions, suicidal or homicidal thoughts, thoughts of self-harm, obsessions, compulsions, paranoia, magical thinking

- Perceptual Disturbances: visual, auditory, or somatic hallucinations, illusions, depersonalization, derealization
