# Overview
Norephinephrine is a [[neurotransmitter|neurotransmitter]] that functions as a stress hormone that increases focus, alertness, and memory retrieval. It's released by the adrenal glands into the bloodstream during the [[fight-flight-freeze-fawn|fight-or-flight]] stress response. 

Substances like cocaine and amphetamines block the norephinephrine transporter (NET), which leads it to build up in the synapses of the nervous system and produce a physical rush. Others like alcohol and opioids suppress norephinephrine, which is why they have sedating and calming effects at first. When chronic substance use begins to cause dependence or addictive behaviors, it means the brain reacts to stress and triggers with massive norepinephrine floods that increases alertness and focus on the disturbance, leading to cravings to get immediate relief.
