# Overview
The Uehiro Oxford Institute (UOI) is a research center that replaced the Oxford Uehiro Centre for Practical Ethics as of 2024 at the University of Oxford. It's funded by the Uehiro Foundation on Ethics and Education. 

Researchers have explored topics related to [[substance-use-disorder|Substance Use Disorder (SUD)]] like the debate between "judgment-shifts" or failure of self-control under [[free-will|free will]], or the disruption of the cognitive link between wanting something and liking it that is often conflated when discussing [[dopamine|dopamine]] as a "pleasure molecule" rather than a reward molecule. They manage a project called Ethics of Behavioural Influence and Prediction (EBIP) which explores the ethics of using neuroscientific interventions to manage crime related to substance use. They also discuss the idea of "cognitive enhancement," where something like [[caffeine|caffeine]] is celebrated as a daily ritualized [[stimulant|stimulant]] that enhances performance while using other substances is heavily penalized.
