# Overview
Risk factors are any attributes or conditions that increase the probability of an adverse outcome happening, particularly developing a new condition or disease. The opposite of risk factors are [[protective-factors|protective factors]], which are gaining more attention in strengths-based therapeutic modalities and [[recovery-capital|recovery capital]] frameworks.

The largest risk factors for the global disease burden include:

- High Blood Pressure: the single largest risk factor that contributes to strokes and heart attacks

- High Fasting Blood Sugar: increases the risks for developing Type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease

- High Body Mass Index: although BMI has received plenty of deserved pushback for painting an incomplete picture of health, it still points to a very real trend in obesity, which contributes to cardiovascular disease and a whole host of other health issues.

- Tobacco Use: Smoking is the leading preventable cause of death in the world, given that it can lead to cancers, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and other conditions.

- Alcohol and Substance Use: Substance use is the primary risk factor for individuals ages 15-49, with alcohol in particular being widely used and leading to liver cirrhosis, mental health crises, accidents, and cardiovascular stress.

- Dietary Risks: Diets high in sodium or low in whole grains and fruits are more likely to lead to health issues

- Inactivity: Many more jobs and lifestyles now are sedentary, which has a lot of negative consequences for one's healthspan and metabolic health

- Air Pollution: Both outdoor pollution and indoor smoke can increase the chance of developing lower respiratory infections and systemic inflammation

- Chemical and Heavy Metal Exposure: This is especially salient with lead pollution, which has been highly damaging and continues to be an issue in developing and developed countries alike.
