# Overview
The power of attorney (POA) is a legal document that gives someone the authority to act on another person's behalf. In legal terms, the principal grants authority to an agent or attorney-in-fact. 

Individuals with substance use disorders can designate a medical POA to make decisions for them in case they get incapacitated as a result of severe intoxication, withdrawal, overdose, or coma. The proxy who holds the POA might be given an advance directive that specifies preferences beforehand around facilities, programs, and medications. They would also have the authorization to obtain records and coordinate with medical staff, which can be vital in emergency situations. However, POAs in the United States cannot be used to force someone into substance use treatment if they are conscious and able to protest; involuntary commitment laws and guardianship/conservatorship processes are separate from POAs and differ by jurisdiction.

There are other kinds of POAs too. A financial POA allows someone to make financial decisions on another person's behalf, such as paying bills, managing mortgages, and handling banking. Someone who enters long-term treatment or fears they might be otherwise incapacitated can designate a proxy through a financial POA to ensure continuity in their financial commitments.
