# Overview
Danshukai is the largest self-help and mutual-aid organization in Japan for those struggling with alcohol use disorder or [[dependence|dependence]]. 

Unlike [[twelve-step|12-step]] groups that prioritize individual [[recovery|recovery]] journeys and anonymity to make a bubble away from daily life, Danshukai actively encourages family members to participate in meetings and recovery alongside the person struggling with alcohol use, since Japanese perspectives of alcoholism traditionally see it as a family issue requiring group healing. Participants use their real names and might even have membership pins. The core idea of Danshukai is "one-day abstinence," or focusing on daily commitments towards social reintegration while avoiding alcohol and pursuing shinsei, or a "New Life" model. There isn't the same emphasis on personal surrender in recognition of powerlessness to a Higher Power's will that is found in AA. Danshukai also keeps a centralized body with formal localized chapters while AA chapters can be formed anywhere while using The Big Book text as a guide.

# Historical Context
The term literally translates as dan = "stop", shu = "liquor", and kai ("society"): "stop liquor society." It was founded in 1953 after [[nihon-kinshu-domei|Nihon Kinshu Domei ("Japanese Temperance Union")]] brought Alcoholics Anonymous (AA) to Japan, but Japanese recovery communities felt it had to be adopted to their culture.
