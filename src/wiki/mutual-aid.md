# Overview
Mutual aid describes a system where members of a community voluntarily share resources and support each other. 

Common examples of mutual aid include:

- Community Fridges and Pantries: public locations where anyone can take or leave food and hygiene products, often without verification.

- Community Kitchens and Neighborhood Cookouts: locations that provide free, hot meals on a regular basis

- Free Stores and Clothing Swaps: places that collect clothes, household goods, books, baby supplies, and other common items and distribute them for free.

- Lending Libraries: community-managed inventories of goods shared among neighbors, ranging from books to medical equipment

- Community Gardens: plots cultivated by a community to provide fresh produce for the surrounding households.

- Bail Funds: money pooled together by a community to pay bail that individuals can't afford

Within recovery spaces, mutual aid networks play an incredibly large role. Traditional 12-step fellowships like Alcoholics Anonymous (AA) and Narcotics Anonymous (NA) are some of the most recognizable mutual aid networks worldwide. Other programs in the recovery space that are built around mutual aid include SMART Recovery, LifeRing Secular Recovery, Women for Sobriety, Refuge Recovery, Recovery Dharma, and many others. Peer-led nonprofit or community programs can also facilitate material exchanges, like Syringe Services Programs (SSPs) and [[drug-users-unions|Drug User Unions.]]
