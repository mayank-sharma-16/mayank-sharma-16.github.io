# Overview
Valence allocation refers to the way positive or negative value, or [[valence|valence]] is assigned to choices or experiences. 

In the context of substance use, the brain experiences a massive flood of [[dopamine|dopamine]] to signal a positive reward as a result of using substances that causes the brain to move positive valence away from natural rewards from the activities of everyday life. Instead, positive valence is assigned to the substance and the environmental cues around it. This happens through specialized neural circuits, namely the dopaminergic reward circuit in the [[ventral-tegmental-area|ventral tegmental area (VTA)]], the amygdala, and the nucleus accumbens.
