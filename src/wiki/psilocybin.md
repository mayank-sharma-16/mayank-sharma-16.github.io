# Overview
Psilocybin is a natural psychedelic found in over 200 fungi, or [[magic-mushrooms|magic mushrooms."]] Like alcohol and marijuana, it has a history going back thousands of years in cultures across the world.

Short-term effects of psilocybin include:

- Sensory Distortion: enhanced visual perception with vivid colors and moving patterns, synesthesia, shifts in perception of space and time

- Emotional Amplification: euphoria (good trip), paranoia and anxiety (bad trip)

- Autonomic Stimulation: increase in heart rate and blood pressure

- Mydriasis (Dilated Pupils)

- Gastrointestinal Distress: nausea, upset stomach, vomiting

- Motor Changes: Muscle weakness, tremors, chills, heaviness, sweating, shivering

Long-term effects include:

- Increased Neuroplasticity: breaking repetitive thinking patterns by increasing neuroplasticity in the brain's Default Mode Network (DMN)

- Improved Mood: evidence shows that clinical delivery of psilocybin can improve the symptoms of Major Depressive Disorder, treatment-resistant depression, and anxiety

- Reduction in Compulsive Behaviors: reduc

- Increased Openness

- Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD): a rare condition where someone experiences visual distortions for months or years after use

- Psychotic Episode: individuals who have a history of or vulnerability to psychotic disorders might have an episode triggered as a result of use
