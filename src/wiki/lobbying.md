# Overview
Lobbying includes activities that attempt to influence policy decisions made by government officials. That can include meeting lawmakers to give a pitch, providing public testimony at hearings, drafting legislation for lawmakers to use, producing research papers and policy briefs, organizing public demonstrations, and purchasing ads to shape public discourse. 

Within addiction recovery and treatment, there are many organizations with different agendas:

- American Society of Addiction Medicine (ASAM): representing physicians and clinicians with input on legislation like Modernizing Opioid Treatment Access Act (MOTAA)

- National Association of Addiction Treatment Providers (NAATP): representing substance use treatment facilities and maintaining an active Political Action Committee (PAC)

- Shatterproof: a national non-profit advocating that mobilizes grassroots support for expanding community harm reduction resources, improving medical training on substance use, and structural parity on healthcare coverage

- Hazelden Betty Ford Foundation: one of the oldest non-profits in the United States that provides addiction treatment, and it also lobbies for clinical research, the behavorial health workforce, and advancing treatment models.
