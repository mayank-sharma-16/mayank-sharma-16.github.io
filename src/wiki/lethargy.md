# Overview
The National Cancer Institute defines "lethargy" as:

*A condition marked by drowsiness and an unusual lack of energy and mental alertness. It can be caused by many things, including illness, injury, or drugs.* [^0]

I also tend to use this definition when I speak, but it was more commonly used in the past to describes one's state of consciousness. As the Cleveland Clinic states,

*Lethargy is a symptom that involves an unusual decrease in consciousness. It’s different from just being drowsy or sleepy. Decreases in your level of consciousness can involve a change in your mental state. That means you may be confused or have trouble remembering things, too.*

*Lethargy comes from two Ancient Greek words: “Lethe," meaning “forgetful,” and “argos,” meaning “idle.” As those two terms describe, someone who’s lethargic moves slowly or is difficult to rouse and has trouble thinking, concentrating or remembering.* [^1]

As with many terms related to consciousness, we might be able to gesture at certain stages relative to others as being more lethargic, but it's been difficult to create definitions that lead to clear standards, so it's not found too often in modern literature as a technical term. You're far more likely to see it used to describe the way someone feels on vacation or after a long night than in a way that intentionally refers to a clinically significant disruption in brain function.
