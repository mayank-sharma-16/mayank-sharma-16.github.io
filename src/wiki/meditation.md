# Overview
Meditation is one of the most popular coping skills and self-care exercises in any context, both inside and outside of recovery and wellness spaces. The mindfulness that's cultivated through meditation can improve impulse control, distress tolerance, and emotional regulation, all of which are common targets in treatment settings. It's also just a way to calm your body's nervous system when you're stressed or tense that anyone can use. Cultures across the world have practiced meditation in some form for thousands of years, so there's an incredibly rich and diverse set of traditions to explore. Some of the scientific evidence about meditation concludes that it:

- reduces intensity of the [[fight-flight-freeze-fawn|fight or flight]] response

- calms rumination and mind-wandering from the brain's Default Mode Network (DMN)

- increases cortical thickness and gray matter density in regions related to emotional processing and metacognitive awareness

- improvements in selective attention, working memory capacity, and cognitive flexibility

- strengthens impulse control and delay triggers or cravings from an automatic behavioral response

- reduces blood pressure, inflammation, and physical tension

- increases cellular longevity markers
