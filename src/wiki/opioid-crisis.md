# Overview
The "opioid crisis" in the United States refers to a decades-long epidemic of [[opioid|opioid]] use and [[overdose|overdoses]]. About 80,000 people died from overdoses involving opioids in 2023, though fatalities peaked in 2022 and have dropped about 12% year-over-year.

# Historical Context
In the 1990s, the over-prescription of [[painkillers|painkillers]] like OxyContin led to a wave of drug-seeking behavior and overdoses. As restrictions tightened on those around 2010, [[heroin|heroin]] increasingly became a source of overdoses, followed by [[fentanyl|fentanyl]] in 2013. 

Data from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) shows that the peak of the crisis was 2022 with 107,000 overdose deaths per year. As of 2026, that number is 67,000 overdose deaths, showing about 14% drop year-over-year. Deaths that result from [[stimulant|stimulants]] like [[cocaine|cocaine]] and [[methamphetamine|methamphetamine]] have also declined in the same time period, but the sharp reduction in fentanyl deaths is attributed to the widespread distribution of [[naloxone|Naloxone (Narcan)]], expanded access to [[medication-for-opioid-use-disorder|Medication for Opioid Use Disorder (MOUD)]], promotion of [[harm-reduction|harm-reduction]] programs like [[syringe-services-program|syringe service programs (SSPs)]] and [[fentanyl-test-strips|fentanyl test strips]], and local recovery programs funded by opioid settlement funds.
