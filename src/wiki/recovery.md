# Overview
Recovery is a widely used term to mean the opposite of active addiction, but what exactly recovery means varies based on the context. 

# Definitions
Definitions of recovery generally fall into one of two categories: abstinence or [[harm-reduction|harm-reduction]]. Literature and institutions derived from 12-step groups like Alcoholics Anonymous are more likely to declare abstinence from all recreational use of substances to be a necessary, but not sufficient, component to recovery. Other groups like SMART Recovery or medical institutions are more likely to adopt a harm-reduction perspective that encourages any change in substance use patterns towards a person's health goals, however incremental the progress may be.

## Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA)

The Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA), in full self-awareness about the complex nuances to addiction treatment, published a paper called the Working Definition of Recovery:

*"A process of change through which individuals improve their health and wellness, live a self-directed life, and strive to reach their full potential."* [^0]

## Betty Ford Institute Consensus Panel

In 2007, the Betty Ford Institute Consensus Panel defined recovery as:

*"a voluntarily maintained lifestyle characterized by sobriety, personal health, and citizenship"*

## Recovery Science Research Collaborative

In 2017, the Recovery Science Research Collaborative arrived at the definition:

*"Recovery is an individualized, intentional, dynamic, and relational process involving sustained efforts to improve wellness."*
