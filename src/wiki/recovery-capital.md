# Overview
Recovery capital (RC) refers to the sum of resources a person has to sustain recovery for mental health conditions and substance use disorders in particular. The most commonly used domains of RC include:

- Human capital: personal characteristics and qualities, including health, education, coping mechanisms, resilience, a sense of hope, etc.

- Social capital: supportive relationships, like friends, family, peer-support networks, and community ties

- Financial capital: money or assets that can be used to procure goods and services, from basic needs to specialized treatment

- Cultural capital: values, beliefs, or organizational structures of particular faith-based or ethnic backgrounds that play a role in one's identity

- Community capital: systemic and local assets, including accessible facilities, recovery community organizations (RCOs), recovery-friendly policies like [[diversion|diversion programs]], etc.

RC frameworks allow us to identify variables and construct factors with strong predictive power. One of the leading indicators of decreased chances of someone relapsing is the increase in recovery capital over the 90-days following sobriety. That ends up being a more reliable indicator than toxicology patterns or the past frequency of use.

# Tests

## Brief Assessment of Recovery Capital (BARC-10)

The Brief Assessment of Recovery Capital (BARC-10) is a 10-item survey that uses a 6-point Likert scale and is commonly used in clinical settings for tracking patient outcomes [[longitudinal-study|longitudinally]]. Studies show it successfully predicts sustained remission at 6 and 12 month windows.

## Recovery Capital Index (RCI)

The Recovery Capital Index (RCI) contains 68 items and yields a score from 1-100.
