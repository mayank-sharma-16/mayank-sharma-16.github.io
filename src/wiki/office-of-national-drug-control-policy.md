# Overview
The Office of National Drug Control Policy (ONDCP) is part of the Executive Office of the President of the United States and coordinates drug policy across 19 federal agencies. The Director who leads the agency is often referred to as the "Drug Czar" and directly advises the president.

# Historical Context
The Office was established under the Anti-Drug Abuse Act of 1988, and the first director, William Bennett, was appointed in 1989. Initially, it focused heavily on the [[crack-cocaine|crack-cocaine]] epidemic and law enforcement as part of the [[war-on-drugs|War on Drugs]]. Under President Clinton, the agency expanded its focus on High Intensity Drug Trafficking Areas (HIDTA) and Drug-Free Communities (DFC) that gave federal grants to local anti-drug coalitions. After 9/11, the Bush administration looked increasingly at [[narcoterrorism|narcoterrorism]] and its role in funding international terrorist networks. The Obama administration applied a public health-oriented approach that focused on community prevention, expanding [[substance-use-disorder|Substance Use Disorder (SUD)]] treatment access, and reforming a criminal justice system that featured [[mandatory-minimums|mandatory minimums]] and [[mass-incarceration|mass incarceration]]. The next decades involving Presidents Biden and Trump would oversee [[overdose|overdose]] spikes as a result of drugs like [[fentanyl|fentanyl]] that have collectively been called an "opioid crisis."
