# Overview
Manic depression is an outdated term for bipolar disorder, coined initially as "das manisch-depressive Irresein" in German by the psychiatrist [[kraepelinian|Emil Kraepelin]], who noticed that some patients cycled between high energy states (mania) and low moods (depression). "Manic-depressive" was a common diagnosis for patients who were admitted into growing psychiatric hospitals in the early 20th century. Eventually, the term was phased out in favor of "bipolar disorder" as a spectrum of cycling between extreme affects and to separate it more distinctly from [[depression|Major Depressive Disorder]], especially since they respond to different pharmaceutical treatments.
