# Overview
Painkillers are another term for [[analgesia|analgesics]], which reduce or relieve pain and discomfort.

# Etymology
The widespread adoption of the term can be traced back to 1844 to a product called "Vegetable Pain Killer," created by Perry Davis. It was a mix of alcohol, opium, camphor, and myrrh, and Davis marketed it as a cure for cholera, coughs, burns, toothaches, ailments of all kinds. It was sold to soldiers during the US Civil War and used by Christian missionaries worldwide, and eventually the product name became the common term for the product class of analgesics: "painkiller." There has been some scholarly discussion about how the term treats pain as a hostile intruder and medicine as a weapon to fight it.
