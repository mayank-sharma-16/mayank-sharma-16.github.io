# Overview
A metabolite is any intermediate or end product made as the result of [[metabolism|metabolism]]. They might be categorized in the following ways:

- Primary Metabolites directly involved in normal growth and development vs Secondary Metabolites that have important roles that aren't required to sustain basic life

- Active Metabolites that produce biological effects vs Inactive Metabolites that await excretion from the body

Many of the effects caused by substances are actually the result of their metabolites interacting with the body. For instance, consuming cocaine and alcohol together leads to a unique metabolite called cocaethylene which is more potent and toxic to the cardiovascular system than cocaine alone would be. Codeine itself is a weak [[analgesia|analgesic]], but human liver enzymes convert some of it to morphine as a metabolite to produce the opioid effect. [[assays|Immunoassays]] are commonly used to detect drug metabolites in urine samples since detecting the parent drug becomes more difficult as time passes from its usage. [[liquid-chromatography-mass-spectrometry-lc-ms|Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS)]] is capable of far more precise detection of molecules in a sample, so it allows us to measure the ratio of a parent drug to its metabolite for more a complete picture of someone's substance use. It's also why dropping medication raw into a urine sample to pretend you're taking it doesn't work; without passing through the body's metabolic processes, the test doesn't have any metabolites to detect.
