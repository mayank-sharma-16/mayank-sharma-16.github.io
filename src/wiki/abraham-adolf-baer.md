# Overview
Abraham Adolf Baer was a German physician and one of the earliest in Europe to study alcoholism as both a medical and public health issue. 

Baer worked as a chief physician at the Plotzensee prison near Berlin, Germany in 1872, where his observations noted an association between [[criminology|criminal behavior]] and [[alcohol|alcohol]] use. He argued that this demonstrated a threat to public health and safety, which added scientific muster to [[temperance|temperance]] movements, although Baer himself favored moderation and targeted intervention against [[liquor|liquor]] with high concentrations of alcohol.
