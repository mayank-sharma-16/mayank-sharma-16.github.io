# Overview
Prohibitionism describes the movements and ideology that argue that people avoid behaviors if an authority enforces rules against them with strict penalties. 

It's used commonly in the context of the Prohibition Era of the United States (1920-1933) when the Eighteenth Amendment and Volstead Act banned the sale of alcoholic beverages. The attempts to prohibit alcohol have historically been considered a disaster, especially in the way it led to bootlegging in black markets that incentivized organized crime to make deals with corrupt law enforcement.
