# Overview
Atomoxetine is a prescription medication used for [[attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder|Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)]] as a selective norephinephrine reuptake inhibitor (SNRI) that blocks the norephinephrine transporter (NET) from clearing norepinephrine and dopamine out of the space between nerves in the brain's prefrontal cortex to stop their signaling. 

Unlike other ADHD medications, atomoxetine is not a stimulant and lacks the same potential for dependency that stimulant medications have, so it's not a controlled substance either. If someone has a history of struggling with substance use disorders or associated symptoms, professional prescribers may opt to try atomoxetine before other medications that cause dopamine spikes in the brain's reward pathways. A 2017 study published in The American Journal of Psychiatry found that atomoxetine is associated with reducing the risk of consequences related to substance use:

*In adjusted within-individual comparisons, relative to periods in which patients did not receive ADHD medication, male patients had 35% lower odds of concurrent substance-related events when receiving medication (odds ratio=0.65, 95% CI=0.64–0.67), and female patients had 31% lower odds of concurrent substance-related events (odds ratio=0.69, 95% CI=0.67–0.71).*

*Moreover, male patients had 19% lower odds of substance-related events 2 years after medication periods (odds ratio=0.81, 95% CI=0.78–0.85), and female patients had 14% lower odds of substance-related events 2 years after medication periods (odds ratio=0.86, 95% CI= 0.82–0.91). Sensitivity analyses supported most findings but were less consistent for long-term associations among women.*
