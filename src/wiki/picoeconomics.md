# Overview
Picoeconomics is a branch of behavioral economics that studies internal motivations within individuals using the principles of microeconomics. 

Instead of viewing addiction as a failure of logic or a disease, picoeconomics explains addiction as a series of successive selves bargaining beween immediately [[gratification|gratifying]] rewards and long-term rewards. When both types of rewards are far away, an individual might express a preference for long-term rewards like health, relationships, and career success. However, when the immediate gratification option comes closer to being available, our brains place a massive spike on its value. This is called hyperbolic [[discounting|discounting]] and explains why those struggling with substance use can be sincerely trying to change yet end up in cycles anyways.
