# Overview
Maladaptive overcontrol is a coping mechanism marked by suppressing emotions, chronic isolation, hypervigilance, and rigidity in forms like perfectionism. 

The standard Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) designed by Marsha Linehan was designed for undercontrolled coping styles that involve emotional impulsivity and externalizing distress, particularly those observed in patients with chronic suicidality and [[borderline-personality-disorder|borderline personality disorder (BPD)]]. Thomas Lynch recognized that many clients and patients actually struggled with what he termed "maladaptive overcontrol" instead, though society often praised or accepted their coping style because it can appear to be the result of self-discipline and compliance rather than suppression and cognitive rigidity. Lynch developed Radically Open Dialectical Behavior Therapy (RO DBT) to build openness and social connectedness in those with maladaptive overcontrol.

# Commentary
Anecdotally, I've found that the bias towards undercontrolling coping styles in treatment settings is enormous. Some of the bias comes from the model of addicts as those who impulsively indulge in instant gratification over long-term consequences as a general pattern in their lives, and so they must be taught to develop coping skills that allow for them to delay impulsive acts. Overcontrolling coping mechanisms often look like strict internal rules and masking affects that are crafted to avoid the vulnerability that comes with impulsively revealing oneself. In these cases, substance use is often an attempt to address the pain of chronic loneliness and the stress of a constant inner critic. Without addressing the root problems around vulnerability, openness, and communication, one might remain isolated even if they can manage their impulses and eventually decide it's worth seeking substances for relief.
