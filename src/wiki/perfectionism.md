# Overview
Perfectionism is a personality trait of holding high expectations of oneself and/or others and applying critical evaluations when they are not met.

# Models

## Frost Multidimensional Perfectionism Scale (FMPS)
The Frost Multidimensional Perfectionism Scale (FMPS) was developed in 1990 by Randy O. Frost and models perfectionism through six dimensions:

- Concern over mistakes: interpreting mistakes as failure that will cost respect from others

- Personal standards: high expectations of oneself that have huge impacts on self-esteem and self-worth

- Parental expectations: the perception that one's parents have or had high expectations of them

- Parental criticism: the perception that one's parents were overly critical of mistakes or failures to meet expectations

- Doubts about actions: a constant sense that daily activities or tasks are not being done well enough

- Organization: a strong emphasis on order and precision

## Hewitt & Flett Multidimensional Perfectionism Scale (HF-MPS)

Paul Hewitt and Gordon Flett proposed this model in 1991 to categorize perfectionism based on its interpersonal direction:

- Self-Oriented perfectionism: an individual demands perfectionism from themselves

- Other-Oriented perfectionism: an individual expects perfectionism from others

- Socially prescribed perfectionism: an individual believes others expect perfectionism from them in order to be validated
