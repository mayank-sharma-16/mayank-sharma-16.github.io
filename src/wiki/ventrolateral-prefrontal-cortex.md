# Overview
The ventrolateral [[prefrontal-cortex|prefrontal cortext]] (VLPFC) is a region of the frontal lobe of the brain. The left VLPFC maintains information in working memory and cognitive control; the right VLPFC plays a major role in motor inhibition, or stopping physical actions. 

Chronic substance use is associated with reduced gray matter and hypoactivation in the right VLPFC, which means the physical act of holding oneself back from using substances again is more difficult to do. When exposed to [[triggers|triggers]] or cues for cravings, the VLPFC can become hyperactive and attribute high salience to the triggers, so one's working memory can become focused on obtaining the substance through intense cravings instead of holding long-term benefits of sobriety in mind.
