# Overview
The Maudsley Model of Anorexia Nervosa Treatment for Adults (MANTRA) is an evidence-based psychotherapy used as a first-line treatment for [[anorexia-nervosa|anorexia nervosa (AN)]] in adults in the United Kingdom. MANTRA is delivered as an individual talk therapy involving workbook exercises and motivational interviewing (MI). It uses the Cognitive-Interpersonal Maintenance Model (CIMM), which argues that a combination of intrapersonal and interpersonal factors are involved in restricting AN:

1. Perfectionism/Cognitive Rigidity: those with eating disorders also tend to have higher levels of obsessive-compulsive personality traits and anxiety traits

2. Experiential Avoidance: an avoidant temperament (e.g., restricted emotional expression or struggles with intimacy and identity) is a risk factor for developing and maintaining AN

3. Pro-Anorectic Beliefs: one of the challenges in treatment for eating disorders is that it's common for clients to have positive beliefs about them. The 'Pros and Cons of Anorexia Nervosa' Questionnaire (P-CAN) administered in 2004 found that the positive beliefs about the benefits of AN included a feeling of safety and stifling emotions.

4. Response of Close Others: relatives who express high amounts of criticism, hostility, and emotional involvement with the patient with an eating disorder are associated with worse overall outcomes for the patient. 

MANTRA is a step-by-step treatment program of workbooks, modules, and therapeutic techniques that address the factors identified by CIMM.
