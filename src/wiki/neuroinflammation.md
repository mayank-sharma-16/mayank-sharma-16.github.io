# Overview
Neuroinflammation refers to a type of [[inflammation|inflammation]] that occurs in the central nervous system, including the brain and spine, in response to injury or infections. 

For those struggling with [[substance-use-disorder|Substance Use Disorder (SUD)]], neuroinflammation is a symptom whose presence further increases the risk of [[craving|cravings]] and [[relapse|relapsing]]. Addictive substances bind directly to immune system receptors in the brain and provoke the release of [[cytokines|cytokines]], whose inflammation can be useful for short-term healing but harmful when chronically present. They damage the [[prefrontal-cortex|prefrontal cortex]] that's responsible for decision-making and impulse control while inducing [[anxiety|anxiety]] and [[anhedonia|anhedonia]]. These neurological changes make it harder for someone to resist cravings when they occur, but they can also compromise the commitments and routines of daily life in ways that increase the chance of relapse.
